  • Applies to stage I–II treated with definitive surgery ± chemotherapy. Surveillance is for recurrence in the first 2–3 years and for new primary lung cancers thereafter.
  • CONFLICT on the length of the intensive phase: NCCN-based tables variously phrase the every-6-months chest CT phase as 2 years (Alberta Health Services LU-013, citing NCCN NSCLC Version 7.2024), 2–3 years (NCCN Version 3.2020 table in IJMPO; NCCN Version 3.2023 as restated in JTCVS Open; 2024 PMC review Table 1), or years 1–3 (Breathe 2024 Table 5, NCCN 2023). Frequencies agree; the 2–3-year / years 1–3 phrasing was used here as the most commonly restated current NCCN wording.
  • CONFLICT on when annual low-dose CT starts: an Evolent / payer policy referencing NCCN NSCLC Version 3.2025 states annual LDCT starting 3 years after end of treatment for stage I–II with no history of radiation, whereas Breathe 2024 (NCCN 2023) places annual LDCT in years 4–5 and continuing after year 5. Annual frequency after the intensive phase is consistent across sources.
  • PET/CT is not recommended for routine surveillance.
  • Routine brain MRI surveillance is not recommended for NSCLC (Alberta LU-013; ASCO surveillance guideline). Brain imaging is for symptoms or work-up of suspected recurrence.
  • Circulating biomarkers should not be used as a surveillance strategy for detection of recurrence (ASCO guideline). No routine surveillance labs are specified in the NCCN table — surveillance consists of history & physical and chest CT.
  • More frequent imaging may be required for residual or new radiographic abnormalities.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: NSCLC Version 3.2020 (IJMPO table), Version 7.2024 — June 26, 2024 (Alberta Health Services), and Version 3.2025 (Evolent LDCT policy).

Test / examFrequency
History & physical + low-dose noncontrast CT chestevery 12 months
  • This is the NCCN pathway for primary treatment that included radiotherapy — it applies to stage III (and to stage I–II treated with radiotherapy and stage IV oligometastatic disease treated definitively), not to surgery-only patients.
  • PET/CT is not recommended for routine surveillance. Previous areas treated with RT may show FDG avidity for up to 2 years, so histologic confirmation of apparent recurrent disease is mandatory (NCCN table footnote, Version 3.2020 via IJMPO). Post-chemoradiation fibrosis can mimic relapse on CT — compare with prior scans.
  • Routine brain MRI surveillance is not recommended for NSCLC; brain imaging is symptom- or recurrence-driven.
  • Circulating biomarkers should not be used as a surveillance strategy for detection of recurrence (ASCO guideline). No routine surveillance labs are specified in the NCCN table.
  • More frequent imaging may be required for residual or new radiographic abnormalities.
  • Timing of CT scans within the guideline parameters (e.g. every 3 vs every 6 months in years 1–3) is a clinical decision (NCCN table footnote).
  • An Evolent / payer policy referencing NCCN NSCLC Version 3.2025 states annual LDCT starting 6 years after end of treatment for stage III or IV disease, consistent with switching to annual low-dose CT after the 5-year chest CT phase above.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: NSCLC Version 3.2020 (IJMPO table) and the 2024 PMC review Table 1 restating current NCCN (stage III/IV: every 3–6 months for 3 years, then every 6 months in years 3–5, then annual low-dose CT).

Sources: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443 · www.ijmpo.org · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Stage IV — on systemic therapy (response assessment)

On systemic therapy / maintenance

Test / examFrequency
CT with contrast (response assessment)every 6–12 weeks
Clinical assessment (history & physical)at each treatment visit / cycle
Brain imagingas clinically indicated (known brain metastases or new neurologic symptoms) — no fixed surveillance interval
  • IMPORTANT SCOPE NOTE: this is ON-TREATMENT response assessment, not post-treatment surveillance. NCCN's formal NSCLC Surveillance section covers only curative-intent treatment (stage I–II surgery or radiotherapy, stage III) and stage IV oligometastatic disease with all sites treated definitively — those patients follow the resected and post-chemoradiation variants above, not this one.
  • The every 6–12 weeks figure is NCCN response-assessment language for advanced disease: the JNCCN NSCLC discussion (Version 4.2024) recommends response assessment every 6–12 weeks during maintenance therapy, as restated in a 2025 peer-reviewed review; Alberta Health Services guideline LU-012 (advanced NSCLC, citing NCCN NSCLC Version 7.2024) independently phrases follow-up imaging and assessment as every 6–12 weeks while receiving ongoing treatment.
  • CONFLICT / attribution nuance: the 6–12-week interval is co-cited to ESMO in the Alberta guideline, and payer imaging policies operationalize restaging during systemic therapy as 'every 2 cycles' — treat 6–12 weeks as a range for on-therapy monitoring, individualized to regimen and response, not as a fixed surveillance cadence. NCCN states no post-treatment surveillance table for disseminated stage IV disease off therapy.
  • After completing 2 years of immunotherapy without progression, Alberta LU-012 suggests CT every 3–4 months, with the interval lengthening for patients off systemic therapy ≥5 years — recorded here as a guideline restatement (co-cited to NCCN/ESMO), not a verbatim NCCN surveillance-table row.
  • As in the curative-intent variants, PET/CT is not recommended for routine monitoring/surveillance in this setting.

Sources: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · ahs.ca

  • NCCN's surveillance table places stage IV oligometastatic disease with all sites treated with definitive intent in the same row as stage III and RT-treated stage I–II: H&P + chest CT every 3–6 months for 3 years, then every 6 months for 2 years, then annual low-dose noncontrast chest CT. These bands are identical to the Stage III CRT variant because they are the same table row.
  • This variant covers stage IV disease rendered no-evidence-of-disease by definitive treatment of the primary tumor and every metastatic site (resection and/or ablative radiotherapy) — the oligometastatic/converted-to-NED unit. It is post-treatment surveillance, not on-therapy monitoring.
  • PET/CT and brain MRI are not routinely indicated in surveillance; residual or new radiographic abnormalities may require more frequent imaging, and a mass on CT may warrant PET/CT to distinguish recurrence from radiation change or other benign causes.
  • Disseminated stage IV disease on systemic therapy is a different unit — response assessment every 6–12 weeks — see the Stage IV systemic-therapy variant. NCCN publishes no surveillance schedule for disseminated stage IV disease off therapy.
