Follow-up schedules for melanoma — cutaneous based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

  • No routine imaging for stage 0–IIA; imaging only to evaluate specific signs or symptoms concerning for recurrence/metastasis.
  • Routine blood tests (including LDH) are not recommended for surveillance at any stage.
  • Annual total-body skin examination is lifelong (risk of second primary melanoma); educate on monthly skin self-examination.
  • Per NCCN Melanoma: Cutaneous (clinician guideline v2.2026; Guidelines for Patients based on v2.2025). One review restates the stage IA–IIA exam interval as every 6–12 months for the first 1–2 years then annually; the NCCN follow-up table specifies every 6–12 months for 5 years, then annually as clinically indicated — the table value is used here.

After Year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical exam with emphasis on skin and nodesannually, as clinically indicated
Complete skin examinationannually, lifelong
  • Routine imaging is not recommended beyond 5 years in patients with no evidence of disease; image only for signs/symptoms thereafter.
  • Routine laboratory studies (including LDH) are not recommended for surveillance.
  • Consider more frequent brain MRI in patients with prior brain metastases or high risk for CNS relapse (e.g., stage IIIC or higher).
  • Source variance flagged: NCCN patient-facing summaries and AIM at Melanoma give the years 3–5 exam interval as every 3–12 months, while some reviews (StatPearls) restate it as every 6–12 months; the NCCN patient-guideline wording is used here.
  • Per NCCN Melanoma: Cutaneous (clinician guideline v2.2026; Guidelines for Patients based on v2.2025).

Years 1–2

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency

Cross-sectional imaging (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis or PET/CT) ± brain MRI — considerevery 6–12 months

After Year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical examannually, as clinically indicated
Imagingonly for signs/symptoms — routine imaging not recommended beyond 5 years if no evidence of disease
  • NCCN publishes no separate surveillance table for mucosal melanoma. The NCCN Melanoma guideline directs that mucosal melanoma be followed with the same protocols used for cutaneous melanoma (stated explicitly for vulvovaginal primaries, which are also staged as cutaneous melanoma). The schedule shown is the resected stage IIB–IV cutaneous schedule, which fits most mucosal primaries because they usually present locally advanced; for a genuinely early-stage mucosal primary, apply the stage 0–IIA cutaneous schedule instead.
  • Include surveillance of the primary site itself: anoscopy/digital rectal exam for anorectal, speculum/pelvic exam for vulvovaginal, and nasal/oral endoscopy for sinonasal or oral primaries. Case literature applying the NCCN melanoma guideline to anorectal primaries uses exam every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6–12 months, with imaging every 3–6 months for 2 years, then annually to 5 years.
  • Head and neck mucosal primaries: diagnostic workup and treatment are covered by the NCCN Head and Neck Cancers guideline; the head and neck post-treatment surveillance pattern (see Head & Neck entry) can reasonably be layered onto the primary site alongside the systemic surveillance shown here.
  • Routine blood tests (including LDH) are not recommended for surveillance, as with cutaneous melanoma.

Sources: reference-global.com · jeghers.libguides.com · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · ascopubs.org

Unresectable stage III / IV — monitoring on systemic therapy (no local therapy)

During active systemic therapy

Test / examFrequency
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis or FDG-PET/CT — response assessmentevery 2–6 months during active therapy, then per regimen
  • NCCN Melanoma: Cutaneous (v2.2026) publishes NO on-therapy imaging interval for unresectable stage III/IV disease — its follow-up table is written for patients with no evidence of disease after treatment (see the resected pathways in this app). The interval shown is NOT NCCN text.
  • Source of the interval (single melanoma-specific source): Eviti/NantHealth Imaging Guideline — Cutaneous Melanoma v1.0 (effective 1/1/2026, referenced to NCCN): 'Treatment Monitoring — Systemic Therapy, Stage III–IV: CT chest/abdomen/pelvis or FDG-PET/CT — every 2–6 months during active therapy, then per regimen', with response assessed per RECIST / immune-related criteria; it explicitly covers unresectable or incompletely resected nodal disease and stage III satellite/in-transit disease. Shown here labeled as a payer-guideline interval, per house practice for non-NCCN figures.
  • A second payer source conflicts and is NOT adopted: eviCore's generic cross-disease phase table (ONC-1.2) supports imaging every 2 cycles (≈ every 6–8 weeks) during chemotherapy/immunotherapy — materially more frequent, not melanoma-specific, and not NCCN wording.
