Basal Cell Skin Cancer — Surveillance & Follow-Up Schedule (NCCN)

Follow-up schedules for basal cell skin cancer based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

After treatment

Years 1–5

Test / examFrequency
Complete skin exam (incl. treatment site)every 6–12 months
Imaging — consider if clinical exam is insufficient for following the diseaseas clinically indicated
Follow-up with a dermatologist — if past or imminent solid organ, marrow, or hematopoietic cell transplant; ≥1 cutaneous melanoma in the past 5 years; or ≥4 non-melanoma skin cancers in the past 5 yearsstrongly recommended if criteria met (see note)

After year 5 (lifelong)

Test / examFrequency
Complete skin exam (incl. treatment site)annually, for life
  • Per NCCN Basal Cell Skin Cancer (Version 1.2023) as summarized in current reviews: complete skin exam every 6–12 months for the first 5 years, then at least annually for life. Older NCCN summaries state every 6–12 months for life without the 5-year step-down — either is defensible; the stepped schedule is the more current restatement.
  • After topical treatment of superficial BCC, an additional earlier check at 3–6 months after completing treatment is commonly recommended.
  • No routine imaging or laboratory surveillance is recommended for BCC.
  • Every visit: patient education on sun protection and monthly self-skin-examination; a history of BCC confers high risk of additional keratinocyte carcinomas (30–50% develop another within 5 years), which is the main purpose of lifelong annual exams.

Sources: backoffice.nzscd.org.nz · vcha.org · plasticsurgerykey.com

No local therapy (no surgery, no radiation)

  • NCCN's only follow-up schedule for basal cell skin cancer (BCC-6) — skin examination every 6–12 months — is written for local disease after treatment. For advanced disease on a hedgehog inhibitor or cemiplimab, NCCN states no monitoring interval (BCC-D).

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · www.AZMohsSurgery.com
