  • NCCN does not give a separate numeric interval for low-risk tumors: the general completely-resected schedule above applies, and NCCN states only that less frequent imaging surveillance may be acceptable for low-risk tumors (see GIST-A). No distinct low-risk frequency could be verified from NCCN-based sources — see conflict flag.
  • Conflict flag: the 'every 6–12 months for 5 years' figure frequently quoted for low-risk GIST is ESMO guidance, not NCCN — multiple reviews explicitly attribute the 6–12-month low-risk interval to ESMO while attributing every 3–6 months for ≤5 years, then annually, to NCCN. The ESMO interval is therefore not presented here as the NCCN frequency.
  • Very low-risk lesions: surveillance may not be indicated at all (VA GIST Clinical Pathway, V1.2026), and NCI PDQ notes very low-risk lesions may not need to be imaged as frequently.
  • VA pathway variation: the VA GIST Clinical Pathway (September 2026, V1.2026) operationalizes surveillance as H&P plus CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast every 6 months for 5 years, then annually — within, but narrower than, the NCCN 3–6-month range.
  • No routine surveillance labs or endoscopy are specified in the NCCN follow-up schedule; follow-up is H&P and imaging only.
  • Conflict flag (versions): older NCCN versions (e.g., Version 2.2024, restated in HealthHelp imaging policies) phrased imaging as every 3–6 months for 3–5 years, then annually thereafter; current NCCN Version 1.2026 (January 13, 2026) states every 3–6 months for 5 years, then annually, with surveillance individualized after 10 years. The current wording is used here.
  • NCCN version note: GIST Version 1.2026 (January 13, 2026); the same H&P plus CT/MRI every 3–6 months for 5 years, with an option for less frequent imaging in low-risk tumors, is restated from Version 1.2025 in the AME review.

Beyond year 10

Test / examFrequency
History and physical (H&P) and imagingindividualize surveillance
  • More frequent imaging surveillance may be required for high-risk patients after discontinuing TKI therapy (adjuvant imatinib) — stated explicitly in restatements of NCCN Principles of Imaging (Version 2.2024, via HealthHelp); Version 1.2026 retains 'more frequent imaging surveillance may be required' language in GIST-F. Expert follow-up reviews suggest intensifying to CT/MRI every 3–4 months for the first 2 years after imatinib discontinuation, when recurrence risk is highest, but that specific 3–4-month interval is expert/ESMO-flavored guidance, not an NCCN-stated frequency.
  • Adjuvant imatinib is preferred for patients with significant (moderate or high) risk of recurrence; available data support adjuvant imatinib for high-risk disease for at least 3 years (overall-survival benefit), with Version 1.2026 also citing at least 6 years for disease-free-survival benefit. Surveillance on the schedule above continues during and after adjuvant therapy.
  • Conflict flag (versions): older NCCN versions (e.g., Version 2.2024) and reviews restating them give abdominal/pelvic CT every 3–6 months for 3–5 years, then annually; current NCCN Version 1.2026 (January 13, 2026) states H&P and imaging every 3–6 months (every 3 months if high risk) for 5 years, then annually, with surveillance individualized after 10 years. The current wording is used here.
  • No routine surveillance labs or endoscopy are specified in the NCCN follow-up schedule; follow-up is H&P and imaging only.
  • FDG-PET/CT is not a substitute for diagnostic CT; in the Principles of Imaging it is reserved for clarifying ambiguous CT/MRI findings rather than routine surveillance.

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · gist.amegroups.org · www.healthhelp.com · www.ejcancer.com · www.cancer.va.gov · www.cancer.gov

Unresectable / recurrent / metastatic — on TKI therapy (on-treatment monitoring)

While on systemic therapy

Test / examFrequency
CT or MRI abdomen/pelvis with contrastevery 8–12 weeks
PET/CTnot a substitute for CT/MRI; may give an indication of imatinib efficacy after 2–4 weeks of therapy when a rapid readout of activity is necessary; routine long-term PET/CT follow-up is rarely indicated
  • On-treatment monitoring, not post-treatment surveillance: this is the response-assessment cadence NCCN states for unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic GIST receiving a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (imatinib or later-line TKI), per the GIST-4 footnote in the NCCN Guidelines for Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (v2.2022; also v1.2022): 'Diagnostic abdominal/pelvic CT or MRI with contrast is indicated every 8–12 weeks.'
  • Per the same guideline footnote, imaging frequency may be decreased for patients who are responding to therapy or have stable disease.
  • Variation flag: some institutional pathways (e.g., the VA oncology pathway) operationalize metastatic GIST imaging at every 3–6 months; the cadence encoded here is the one stated in the NCCN guideline text itself (every 8–12 weeks).
  • NCCN publishes no separate surveillance table for metastatic GIST resected to no evidence of disease; after complete resection, follow-up follows the risk-based post-resection schedules in the other GIST variants.
