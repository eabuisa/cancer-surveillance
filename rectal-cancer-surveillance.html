Rectal Cancer — Surveillance & Follow-Up Schedule (NCCN)

Follow-up schedules for rectal cancer based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

Open the interactive Rectal Cancer schedule

After TME surgery

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
History and physical examinationevery 3–6 months
CEAevery 3–6 months
CT chest/abdomen/pelvisevery 6–12 months, for a total of 5 years (stage II–III)
Colonoscopyat 1 year after surgery (at 3–6 months if no complete preoperative colonoscopy); if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year; if no advanced adenoma, repeat in 3 years, then every 5 years

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
History and physical examinationevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
CEAevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
CT chest/abdomen/pelvisevery 6–12 months, for a total of 5 years (stage II–III)
Colonoscopyper 1-year findings — if no advanced adenoma, repeat at 3 years, then every 5 years; if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year
  • Bands above are the stage II–III operative (transabdominal resection / TME) schedule in NCCN Rectal Cancer REC-10. Stage I with full surgical staging after TME: colonoscopy only — at 1 year, then per adenoma findings (repeat in 1 year if advanced adenoma; in 3 years then every 5 years if none); other testing only if symptoms or suspected recurrence.
  • Stage IV resected: same history/physical and CEA schedule, but CT chest/abdomen/pelvis every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6–12 months for a total of 5 years (REC-10).
  • No routine FDG-PET/CT — FDG-PET/CT is not recommended for operative surveillance (REC-10).
  • ctDNA is not recommended for surveillance (NCCN footnote, Rectal Cancer v2.2026 REC-10).
  • Routine CT scanning is not recommended beyond 5 years.
  • CEA surveillance is intended for patients who are potential candidates for resection of isolated metastases.
  • Conflict flag — proctoscopy after TME: current NCCN REC-10 (v2.2026) does NOT list routine proctoscopy after transabdominal/TME resection; the proctoscopy schedule in REC-10 (every 3–6 months x 2 years, then every 6 months to 5 years, with EUS or MRI) applies to transanal local excision only. Some secondary summaries (e.g., a Taipei NCCN restatement listing 'rigid proctoscopy (optional) every 3–4 months x 2 years, then every 6 months to 5 years' and ASCRS guidance on proctoscopy after resection with an anastomosis) retain proctoscopy as optional/consider — it is not in the current NCCN operative table, so it is not listed as a routine item here.
  • NCCN version note: verified in NCCN Rectal Cancer clinician guideline Version 2.2026 (April 7, 2026), REC-10 'Surveillance Following Operative Management', and in the 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer, Guide 4 'Surveillance after abdominal surgery'.

Watch-and-wait / organ preservation after total neoadjuvant therapy

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
History and physical examinationevery 3–6 months
CEAevery 3–6 months
Digital rectal examination (DRE) and proctoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopyevery 3–4 months
MRI rectumevery 6 months, for up to 3 years
CT chest/abdomenevery 6–12 months, for a total of 5 years (add CT pelvis once MRI rectum is no longer being done)
Colonoscopyat 1 year following completion of therapy; if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year; if no advanced adenoma, repeat in 3 years, then every 5 years

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
History and physical examinationevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
CEAevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
Digital rectal examination (DRE) and proctoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopyevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
MRI rectumevery 6 months in year 3 only — up to 3 years total, then stop
CT chest/abdomen/pelvisevery 6–12 months, for a total of 5 years — include pelvis once MRI rectum stops (after year 3)
  • Applies to nonoperative management after a clinical complete response (or near-complete response evolving to complete response) to total neoadjuvant therapy; total surveillance duration is 5 years from the post-treatment response assessment.
  • No routine FDG-PET/CT for surveillance of nonoperatively managed rectal cancer.
  • ctDNA is not recommended for surveillance (NCCN footnote in the Rectal Cancer guideline).
  • NCCN version note: verified in NCCN Rectal Cancer clinician guideline Version 2.2026 (April 7, 2026), REC-10A 'Surveillance Following Nonoperative Management', and in the 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer, Guide 5 'Surveillance after non-surgical treatment'; the same intervals are tabulated in JAMA Network Open 2024 (Table 1, NCCN surveillance for nonoperative management) and restated in Curr Oncol 2025.
  • Wording note: REC-10A lists 'history and physical examination every 3–6 months for 2 years and then every 6 months for a total of 5 years' and the identical interval for CEA; the patient Guide 5 combines these as 'physical exam and CEA' with the same intervals.

After local excision (transanal)

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
Proctoscopy with endorectal ultrasound or pelvic MRI with contrastevery 3–6 months
Colonoscopyat 1 year after surgery; if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year; if no advanced adenoma, repeat in 3 years, then every 5 years

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
Proctoscopy with endorectal ultrasound or pelvic MRI with contrastevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
Colonoscopyper 1-year findings — if no advanced adenoma, repeat at 3 years, then every 5 years; if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year
  • Applies when transanal local excision is the definitive treatment (classically low-risk pT1, N0 disease). Total surveillance duration is 5 years (NCCN Rectal Cancer v2.2026 REC-10; 2026 patient guideline Guide 3).
  • REC-10's local-excision row lists proctoscopy (with EUS or MRI) and colonoscopy only — it does not list a routine history/physical, CEA, or CT chest/abdomen/pelvis schedule for this pathway, unlike the stage II–IV transabdominal-resection rows on the same page.
  • If local-excision pathology shows adverse features or upstaging (e.g., high-risk T1 or T2), completion treatment pathways supersede this surveillance schedule.
  • Advanced adenoma is defined in the guideline footnote as villous polyp, polyp >1 cm, or high-grade dysplasia.
  • NCCN version note: verified in NCCN Rectal Cancer clinician guideline Version 2.2026 (April 7, 2026), REC-10 'Transanal local excision only' column, and in the 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer, Guide 3 'Surveillance after transanal local excision' — both give proctoscopy with EUS or MRI every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6 months to 5 years, and colonoscopy at 1 year.

After polypectomy (low-risk malignant polyp)

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
Physical examination and proctoscopyevery 3–6 months, for the first 2 years
Colonoscopyat 1 year after polypectomy
  • Applies to low-risk malignant polyps (pT1) removed by polypectomy when no further surgery is undertaken (NCCN Rectal Cancer v2.2026 REC-10, 'Low-risk polyps removed by polypectomy' column).
  • REC-10 specifies no surveillance items beyond year 2 for this pathway and no routine CEA, CT, EUS, or MRI — local endoscopic surveillance only.
  • If the polyp shows high-risk features, transabdominal resection or other treatment pathways supersede this schedule.
  • NCCN version note: verified in NCCN Rectal Cancer clinician guideline Version 2.2026 (April 7, 2026), REC-10, and in the 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer ('Surveillance after polypectomy': colonoscopy in 1 year; physical exams with proctoscopy every 3 to 6 months for 2 years).

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · WWW.NCCN.ORG

After endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
Flexible sigmoidoscopyevery 6 months — first surveillance exam 3–6 months after ESD
Endorectal ultrasound or pelvis MRI with contrastevery 3–6 months

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
Flexible sigmoidoscopyevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years from ESD
Endorectal ultrasound or pelvis MRI with contrastevery 6 months, to complete 5 years
  • Applies after curative ESD of a low-risk T1 rectal cancer (NCCN Rectal Cancer v2.2026, Principles of Surgery REC-C 'Surveillance After ESD'; REC-10 refers ESD surveillance to REC-C).
  • Sequencing detail (REC-C): a second follow-up endoscopy is recommended 3–6 months after the first surveillance exam, or colonoscopy if 1 year from ESD.
  • If the resected lesion does not meet curative-resection criteria, a multidisciplinary team should decide between surgery and intensive surveillance (REC-C).
  • NCCN version note: verified in NCCN Rectal Cancer clinician guideline Version 2.2026 (April 7, 2026), REC-C 6 of 9, and in the 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer, Guide 2 'Five-year surveillance after ESD' (flexible sigmoidoscopy every 6 months; EUS or pelvis MRI with contrast every 3–6 months in years 1–2, then every 6 months in years 3–5).

Stage IV, resected — no evidence of disease

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
History and physical examinationevery 3–6 months
CEAevery 3–6 months
CT chest/abdomen/pelvisevery 3–6 months for 2 years
Colonoscopyat 1 year after surgery (at 3–6 months if no complete colonoscopy at diagnosis); if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year; if no advanced adenoma, repeat in 3 years, then every 5 years

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
History and physical examinationevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
CEAevery 6 months, for a total of 5 years
CT chest/abdomen/pelvisevery 6–12 months, for a total of 5 years
Colonoscopyper 1-year findings — if no advanced adenoma, repeat at 3 years, then every 5 years; if advanced adenoma, repeat in 1 year
  • Stage IV (NED) row of NCCN Rectal Cancer REC-10: after resection of the primary tumor and all metastatic disease with no evidence of disease, surveillance is the same history/physical and CEA schedule as stage II–III, but CT chest/abdomen/pelvis is more frequent in the first 2 years (every 3–6 months, then every 6–12 months to a total of 5 years). Previously this schedule appeared only as a note inside the 'After TME surgery' variant; it is now broken out as its own variant.
  • The 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer present this as Guide 7, 'Surveillance after surgical treatment for stage 4 rectal cancer' (physical exam and CEA every 3–6 months for the first 2 years, then every 6 months for the next 3 years; CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; colonoscopy at 3–6 months if there was no complete colonoscopy at diagnosis, otherwise 1 year after surgery).
  • After a complete response to checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy for dMMR/MSI-H metastatic disease, NCCN directs patients back to surveillance (patient guide, chapter 5, 'Treatment for metastatic rectal cancer').
  • No routine FDG-PET/CT — FDG-PET/CT is not recommended for surveillance (REC-10).
  • ctDNA is not recommended for surveillance (NCCN footnote, Rectal Cancer v2.2026 REC-10).
  • Routine CT scanning is not recommended beyond 5 years.
  • CEA surveillance is intended for patients who are potential candidates for resection of isolated metastases.
  • NCCN version note: REC-10 stage IV (NED) row, NCCN Rectal Cancer clinician guideline Version 2.2026; corroborated by the NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Rectal Cancer, 2026 (Guide 7) and by World J Gastroenterol 2025 (PMC12146933), whose comparison table restates the NCCN stage IV CT interval as every 3–6 months for the first 2 years.

Metastatic dMMR/MSI-H — monitoring on immunotherapy (no local therapy)

On immunotherapy

Test / examFrequency
Re-evaluate disease status — CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast, or chest CT plus abdomen/pelvis MRI with contrast (FDG-PET/CT should not be used)every 2-3 months
  • Applies ONLY to dMMR/MSI-H or POLE/POLD1 ultra-hypermutated (e.g. TMB >50 mut/Mb) tumors treated with checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy, any line, with no prior immunotherapy (NCCN Rectal Cancer v3.2025, REC-F 3 of 13, Continuum of Care — Systemic Therapy for Advanced or Metastatic Disease; retained in v2.2026, REC-18). Pathway footnote: 'Patients should be followed closely for 10 weeks to assess for response.' If disease response, consider discontinuing checkpoint inhibitor after 2 years of treatment.
  • NCCN states NO re-evaluation interval for pMMR/MSS metastatic rectal cancer on chemotherapy (REC-F 1-2 of 13 give regimens and the imaging modality only). The only other interval in the guideline is re-evaluation for conversion to resectable disease every 2 months, and only when conversion to resectability is a reasonable goal (REC-13).
  • After a complete response on immunotherapy, the pathway exits to surveillance following nonoperative management (REC-10A) or surgery ± RT — see the watch-and-wait / nonoperative variants.
