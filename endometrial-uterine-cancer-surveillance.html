  • NCCN Uterine Neoplasms (Version 3.2024, as tabulated in the 2025 FEBRASGO position statement) applies a single examination schedule across stages, with the interval tailored to recurrence risk; low-risk patients sit at the less frequent end of each range.
  • Routine vaginal cytology is NOT recommended — it does not usefully detect vaginal vault recurrence in asymptomatic patients (also an SGO Choosing Wisely item).
  • Routine surveillance imaging is NOT recommended; imaging only if symptoms or examination suggest recurrence.
  • Verbal and written education on recurrence symptoms (vaginal bleeding, pelvic/abdominal pain, cough, dyspnea, weight loss) is an explicit NCCN element of follow-up.
  • Note: the 2025 SGO recommendations differ slightly for low-risk patients (exams every 6–12 months in years 1–2, then annually in years 3–5); shown here is the NCCN schedule.

  • CONFLICT FLAGGED: an NCCN-based surveillance table (FEBRASGO 2025, adapted from Salani 2017 and NCCN Uterine Neoplasms v3.2024) lists scheduled CT abdomen/pelvis every 6 months for the first 3 years, then every 6–12 months for 2 more years, for patients with advanced disease at diagnosis — shown above. However, the 2026 SGO statement's tabulation of the 2024 NCCN guideline lists CT only 'as clinically indicated' even for high-risk patients, and the 2025 SGO recommendations suggest only considering annual CT in years 1–2 for high-risk disease. Practice varies; many clinicians now image only as indicated.
  • Routine vaginal cytology is NOT recommended.
  • High risk = stage III–IV disease or high-risk histology (serous, clear cell, carcinosarcoma, undifferentiated), per the risk definitions used in the SGO/NCCN comparison tables.
  • Most recurrences are symptomatic and occur within the first 3 years; symptom education is an explicit NCCN element of follow-up.

Fertility-sparing progestin therapy — monitoring (no hysterectomy / no local therapy)

On progestin therapy

Test / examFrequency
Endometrial evaluation (hysteroscopy, D&C, or endometrial biopsy)every 3–6 months
Pelvis MRI (preferred)repeat if persistent endometrial carcinoma after 6–9 months of ineffective treatment, especially if considering further fertility-sparing approaches

After complete response

Test / examFrequency
Endometrial sampling (continued surveillance)every 6–12 months
  • NCCN Uterine Neoplasms v2.2026, ENDO-8 (Criteria for Considering Fertility-Sparing Options) — the page's third column is headed 'Monitoring' (renamed from 'Surveillance' in v1.2026): 'Endometrial evaluation every 3–6 mo (either hysteroscopy, D&C, or endometrial biopsy)' during continuous progestin-based therapy. Primary treatment: levonorgestrel IUD (preferred), megestrol, or medroxyprogesterone; consider hysteroscopic tumor evaluation and resection.
  • Eligibility — ALL criteria must be met: grade 1 endometrioid adenocarcinoma (preferred) on hysteroscopy/D&C, confirmed by expert pathology review; disease limited to the endometrium on MRI (preferred) or transvaginal ultrasound; absence of suspicious or metastatic disease on imaging; no contraindications to medical therapy or pregnancy; consultation with a fertility expert prior to therapy; molecular evaluation / inherited cancer risk evaluation. Patients must be counseled that the fertility-sparing option is NOT standard of care.
  • Response branches (ENDO-8): complete response by 6 months → encourage conception with continued surveillance/endometrial sampling every 6–12 months; consider maintenance progestin-based therapy. Endometrial cancer present at 6 months → consider adding dual-progestin therapy [(megestrol or medroxyprogesterone) + levonorgestrel IUD] if not previously added. Endometrial cancer present at 12 months → TH/BSO with staging (preferred by 12 months). TH/BSO with staging is also recommended after childbearing is complete or with progression of disease on endometrial sampling.
  • MRI item is from ENDO-B (Principles of Imaging, Follow-up/Surveillance — Fertility-Sparing Treatment): 'Repeat pelvis MRI (preferred) for patients with persistent endometrial carcinoma after 6–9 months of ineffective treatment, especially if considering further fertility-sparing approaches.' ENDO-B also suggests considering pelvis ultrasound surveillance for patients with ovarian preservation.
  • Separate scenario — NOT covered by this variant and has NO NCCN cadence: advanced/metastatic endometrial cancer not suitable for primary surgery, managed with systemic therapy (ENDO-3, v2.2026: 'Systemic therapy' → 'Re-evaluate for surgical resection and/or RT based on response'). NCCN states no interval, imaging modality, or lab/visit schedule for that scenario; the ENDO-9 surveillance schedule applies only after treatment.

Sources: download.s21i.faimallusr.com · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

No local therapy (no surgery, no radiation)

  • For advanced or unresectable endometrial cancer managed with systemic therapy without hysterectomy, NCCN (ENDO-3) states only that patients be re-evaluated for surgical resection and/or radiation based on response; no monitoring cadence is published. (Fertility-sparing progestin management has its own monitoring pathway, above.)
