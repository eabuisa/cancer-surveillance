Low risk of metastasis — after treatment of primary tumor

Years 1–5

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance imaging — MRI (with contrast) or ultrasound of the liver, or CT chest/abdomen/pelvis (with contrast)every 12 months

After Year 5

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance imagingas clinically indicated
  • Uveal melanoma is a separate NCCN guideline (Melanoma: Uveal) from cutaneous melanoma; surveillance is risk-stratified, not stage-based in the cutaneous sense. Risk is assigned from tumor size and location, histologic features, and molecular prognostic markers — low risk typically means a small tumor with favorable markers (e.g., gene expression profile class 1, disomy 3, EIF1AX mutation).
  • The liver is the dominant — often the only — site of metastasis, so surveillance imaging is liver-focused; MRI is the most sensitive modality. NCCN sets the interval by risk group and leaves the choice among CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, liver MRI, and liver ultrasound open.
  • Intervals per NCCN Melanoma: Uveal v1.2025, as restated in HealthHelp's NCCN-based imaging guideline; corroborated by the British Journal of Cancer (2026) hepatic surveillance cohort review and a Cancers review of NCCN uveal surveillance.

Medium (intermediate) risk of metastasis — after treatment of primary tumor

Years 1–10

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance imaging — MRI (with contrast) or ultrasound of the liver, or CT chest/abdomen/pelvis (with contrast)every 6–12 months

After Year 10

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance imagingas clinically indicated
  • Risk category per NCCN Melanoma: Uveal (v1.2025) is assigned from tumor size/location, histology, and molecular markers (gene expression profile, chromosome 3 and 8q status, BAP1, PRAME, SF3B1); intermediate features (e.g., SF3B1 mutation, medium tumor size) fall in this tier.
  • The liver is the dominant metastatic site; surveillance imaging is liver-focused, with MRI the most sensitive modality. NCCN sets the interval and leaves modality choice open (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, liver MRI, or liver ultrasound).
  • Intervals corroborated by HealthHelp's restatement of NCCN Melanoma: Uveal v1.2025 and peer-reviewed restatements of the NCCN risk-stratified schedule (medium risk: every 6–12 months for 10 years).

High risk of metastasis — after treatment of primary tumor

Years 1–5

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance imaging — MRI (with contrast) or ultrasound of the liver, or CT chest/abdomen/pelvis (with contrast)every 3–6 months

After Year 5

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance imaging — liver-focused (MRI preferred)every 6–12 months, long-term (see note on total duration)
  • High-risk features per NCCN Melanoma: Uveal include gene expression profile class 2, monosomy 3, chromosome 8q gain, BAP1 mutation/loss, PRAME expression, and large tumor size (AJCC T4). The liver is the dominant metastatic site and metastases can appear very late (beyond 10–15 years), which is why surveillance is prolonged.
  • Duration conflict flagged: HealthHelp's restatement of NCCN Melanoma: Uveal v1.2025 phrases the post-year-5 phase as imaging every 6–12 months 'for 10 years' (potentially to year 15), whereas a peer-reviewed restatement of the same NCCN schedule phrases it as every 6–12 months 'up to 10 years' in total. Both sources agree on every 3–6 months for the first 5 years; the total duration should be individualized.
  • Consider referral to a clinical trial of adjuvant therapy and to ocular oncology/medical oncology co-follow-up for high-risk patients; surveillance-detected liver metastases are increasingly managed with liver-directed and systemic therapy, which is the rationale for the intensive early cadence.

Sources: www.healthhelp.com · www.nature.com · www.thefreelibrary.com

No local therapy (no surgery, no radiation)

  • All NCCN follow-up for uveal melanoma applies after treatment of the primary tumor. For metastatic uveal melanoma (for example, on tebentafusp), NCCN states a workup — biopsy, baseline imaging, liver function tests, HLA-A*02:01 typing — but no monitoring interval.

Sources: www.ajmc.com · consultqd.clevelandclinic.org · www.healthhelp.com
