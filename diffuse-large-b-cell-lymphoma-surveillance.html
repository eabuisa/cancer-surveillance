  • Confirm complete response with end-of-treatment PET/CT before entering surveillance.
  • Surveillance imaging is optional, not mandatory: NCCN states it 'may be considered' in asymptomatic patients, capped at CT chest/abdomen/pelvis no more than every 6 months for 2 years, then only as clinically indicated. Routine surveillance PET/CT is not recommended. Some restatements of recent NCCN versions (v5.2024) phrase this as no routine surveillance imaging after CR — flag: practice varies; the 'may be considered, ≤ every 6 months × 2 years' wording follows the NCCN Guidelines for Patients (B-Cell Lymphomas v4.2026).
  • Surveillance CT language in the guideline is directed particularly at patients treated for stage III–IV disease; for limited-stage disease many clinicians omit routine imaging.
  • Minor variance flagged: some secondary summaries taper visits to every 6 months in years 3–5; the NCCN follow-up wording (every 3–6 months for 5 years, then annually) is used here.
  • Monitor for late effects per treatment exposure (e.g., cardiac after anthracyclines, thyroid after neck radiation) per NCCN Survivorship.

After Year 1

Test / examFrequency
Imagingonly as clinically indicated
Clinical follow-upindividualized — relapse after 1 year is uncommon
  • Per NCCN B-Cell Lymphomas v4.2026 (BURK-2), the Burkitt follow-up statement after complete response is an imaging cap only: C/A/P CT with contrast no more often than every 6 months for 1 year after completion of treatment — a shorter cap than DLBCL's 2 years — then only as clinically indicated. Guideline footnote: relapse after 1 year is uncommon, so follow-up should be individualized according to patient characteristics; unlike DLBCL and PMBL, no numeric clinical-visit interval is specified.
  • Conflict flagged: eviCore's oncology imaging guideline (ONC 27.6) states that NCCN supports Burkitt surveillance CT every 6 months for 2 years. The current NCCN BURK-2 text and HealthHelp's NCCN-based B-cell surveillance summary both specify a 1-year cap; the 1-year cap is used here.
  • Older NCCN versions and reviews of them described scheduled clinical follow-up every 2–3 months during the first 1–2 years; that visit cadence no longer appears in the current algorithm and is not shown as a requirement.
  • Routine surveillance PET/CT is discouraged (high false-positive rate, no demonstrated outcome benefit); confirm any suspected relapse with biopsy. Late-effects monitoring per treatment exposure, per NCCN Survivorship.
