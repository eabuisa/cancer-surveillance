  • GI and pancreatic NETs are followed on different intervals after year 1 in NCCN: GI (jejunum/ileum/colon, duodenum, rectum) every 12–24 months to year 10, pancreatic every 6–12 months to year 10. Both start with a 3–12 month post-resection assessment and continue as clinically indicated beyond year 10 — NETs recur late, so surveillance runs to 10 years.
  • Somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-based imaging and FDG-PET/CT are NOT recommended for routine surveillance (NCCN surveillance footnote, restated in the NET-7 table; the 2019 post-resection review likewise states nuclear medicine imaging is not recommended for routine surveillance).
  • Biochemical markers are 'consider, as clinically indicated' in NCCN — not a mandatory routine panel. Chromogranin A is the marker most used; 5-HIAA and pancreatic peptides are for functioning tumours / markers elevated at diagnosis.
  • CONFLICT: the Canadian Cancer Society patient page (citing NCCN Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors Version 2.2024) lists follow-up visits and chromogranin A / 5-HIAA every 3–6 months for the first 3–5 years, which is more frequent than the NCCN surveillance-table wording ('as clinically indicated' for markers; 12–24 / 6–12 monthly imaging after year 1) used here.
  • Earlier surveillance if symptoms develop; more frequent monitoring may be appropriate for high-grade tumours (NCCN footnote, also flagged in the payer restatement of Version 1.2025).
  • No / minimal surveillance for the lowest-risk resected lesions: no surveillance is indicated for appendiceal tumours ≤2 cm without aggressive features, and small (<1 cm), completely resected, low-grade incidental GI / rectal tumours require no additional follow-up — site- and size-specific exceptions to the schedule above.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors Version 1.2025 (HealthHelp imaging restatement), Version 2.2024 (Canadian Cancer Society), Version 4.2018 / 3.2021 surveillance (NET-7) tables with the same 3–12 month then 12–24 month (GI) structure, and the 2019 J Clin Med review table summarising NCCN as 6–12 monthly for pancreatic NET and every 1–2 years for small-intestinal NET after the first year, to 10 years.

  • Lung (and thymus) NETs share the GI-tract surveillance table in NCCN — 3–12 months post-resection, then every 12–24 months to a maximum of 10 years, then as clinically indicated — with chest CT as the primary-site imaging; abdominal ± pelvic imaging is included in that table. Lung NETs can relapse late (>7 years in the 2019 review), supporting the full 10-year duration.
  • Somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-based imaging and FDG-PET/CT are NOT recommended for routine surveillance (NCCN surveillance footnote).
  • Earlier surveillance if symptoms develop; more frequent monitoring may be appropriate for high-grade / atypical tumours.
  • CONFLICT: the 2019 J Clin Med review (Table 3) summarises the NCCN frequency for lung NETs after the first year as 6–12 monthly, whereas the NCCN surveillance table itself (NET-7, Versions 4.2018 / 3.2021) and the HealthHelp restatement of NCCN Version 1.2025 both give every 12–24 months for 10 years for lung/thymus primaries. The 12–24 month interval was used here as the more current, direct restatement of the NCCN table.
  • CONFLICT: the American Cancer Society lung NET patient guide describes visits about every 3 months in the first year, then every 6 months, then at least annually after 5 years — a patient-facing simplification that does not match the NCCN surveillance-table intervals above.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors Version 1.2025 (HealthHelp imaging restatement) and the Version 4.2018 / 3.2021 NET-7 surveillance table; the 2019 review summarises ENETS / NCCN / CommNETs-NANETS follow-up for resected lung NETs.

After year 1

Test / examFrequency
EGD — gastric NETevery 1–3 years thereafter (type 1: every 2–3 years; type 2: as clinically indicated — see notes)
Endoscopy with rectal MRI or endorectal ultrasound — rectal NET, tumor 1 cm to <2 cmas clinically indicated after the 6- and 12-month checks
  • These endoscopic items are IN ADDITION TO the cross-sectional surveillance in the 'Well-differentiated GI/pancreatic NET, after resection' variant; NCCN lists them as site-specific local follow-up in the same surveillance table (Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors, restated for Version 1.2025).
  • Gastric conflict flag: the NCCN surveillance table phrases post-year-1 gastric EGD generically as every 1–3 years, while the NCCN gastric treatment pathways (restated in Medscape's NCCN summary) narrow it by type — type 1: endoscopic surveillance at 1 year, then every 2–3 years; type 2: at 1 year, then as clinically indicated (MEN1 context). Both formulations are shown; neither was silently dropped.
  • Rectal NET <1 cm, completely resected: no surveillance is required (NCCN, restated in the AGA expert review) — the 6- and 12-month schedule applies only to tumors 1 cm to <2 cm. eviCore's imaging policy phrases the rectal item as MRI pelvis at 6 and 12 months and, if clear, no further imaging.
  • Not encoded — duodenal NET: the NCCN surveillance table (via the HealthHelp Version 1.2025 restatement) also lists duodenal endoscopy every 3–12 months for 1 year, then annually thereafter, but no second independent source stating that interval could be found, so the duodenal interval was left out rather than encoded on a single source.

Sources: www.healthhelp.com · www.cghjournal.org · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · emedicine.medscape.com

  • Per NCCN Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors (JNCCN restatement of Version 2.2021, 'Surveillance of Well-Differentiated Grade 3'): surveillance consists of history & physical examination and imaging (abdominal/pelvic MRI with contrast or multiphasic CT; chest CT as clinically indicated) every 12–24 weeks for the first 2 years, then every 6–12 months thereafter, for up to 10 years. Well-differentiated NET — unresectable / metastatic, on systemic therapy or observation (no local therapy)

Ongoing monitoring — on systemic therapy (SSA, targeted therapy, PRRT, chemotherapy) or observation

Test / examFrequency
Imaging — multiphasic CT or MRI of abdomen/pelvis (involved sites); chest CT as clinically indicatedevery 3–12 months
Tumor markers (e.g. chromogranin A; site-specific markers as applicable)every 3–12 months
  • NCCN's clinician guideline (Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors) publishes NO monitoring cadence for unresectable/metastatic NET on therapy — its follow-up tables are post-resection (see the resected pathways in this app). The interval shown is NOT from an NCCN algorithm or follow-up table.
  • Source of the 3–12-month envelope (best-supported cadence): NCCN Guidelines for Patients, Neuroendocrine Tumors 2025 (based on NCCN Neuroendocrine and Adrenal Tumors v2.2025) lists abdomen/pelvis multiphasic CT or MRI 'every 12 weeks to 12 months' with chest CT as clinically indicated; the NANETS/SNMMI PRRT procedure standard, restating NCCN, likewise gives follow-up imaging and markers 'every 3–12 mo' based on clinical presentation. Where in the envelope a patient sits is set by grade, disease burden, and therapy.
  • Therapy-specific payer cadences conflict and are NOT adopted here: eviCore ONC-15 suggests CT every 3 months on somatostatin analogs vs every 2 cycles (6–8 weeks) on chemotherapy — a split that matches eviCore's generic cross-disease phase table rather than NET-specific NCCN text — and HealthHelp documents variously state every 12 weeks for 12 months or a 3–12-month range. Those are payer policies, not NCCN recommendations.
  • Poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) on chemotherapy is NOT covered by this variant: no NCCN cadence exists for it either (payer documents range from every 2 cycles to every 6–16 weeks).
