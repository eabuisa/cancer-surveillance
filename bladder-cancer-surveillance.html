Bladder Cancer — Surveillance & Follow-Up Schedule (NCCN)

Follow-up schedules for bladder cancer based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

Test / examFrequency

  • NCCN Bladder Cancer Version 1.2026 (JNCCN): low-risk — cystoscopy at months 3 and 12, then yearly up to 5 years; after 5 years only if clinically indicated. Intermediate/high-risk — cystoscopy + cytology every 3–6 months for the first 2 years, then at longer intervals; the high-risk cadence in years 1–4 (every 3 months, then every 6 months) follows the NCCN 2025 follow-up table as summarized in the ASO/ASCO review.
  • Upper tract imaging beyond baseline is NOT indicated for low-risk NMIBC without symptoms.
  • Point of divergence: for intermediate-risk disease, NCCN suggests upper tract imaging as clinically indicated, whereas AUA/SUO recommends scheduled imaging every 1–2 years.
  • PET/CT is not recommended for NMIBC. Urine molecular marker tests are available but do not replace cystoscopy/cytology.

Sources: jnccn.org · www.springermedizin.de · healthhelp.com

  • Core schedule per NCCN Bladder Cancer Version 1.2026 (JNCCN narrative of the BL-E follow-up table): CTU/MRU at 3 and 12 months in year 1, then annually years 2–5; renal ultrasound annually years 5–10; cytology/urethral wash may be considered every 6–12 months for the first 2 years; renal function and LFTs every 3–6 months in year 1, then annually years 2–5; vitamin B12 annually after the first year (individualize).
  • Chest imaging cadence (every 3–6 months years 1–2, then annually to year 5) follows the NCCN follow-up table as summarized in payer policy based on NCCN v3.2023 — imaging intervals vary slightly between guideline versions; verify against the current BL-E table.
  • Vitamin B12 monitoring is especially relevant after urinary diversion using bowel segments.
  • FDG PET/CT (category 2B) only when metastatic disease is suspected.
  • Also applies to stage IVA/IVB disease rendered NED by consolidative cystectomy after response to systemic therapy — BL-9/BL-10 route these patients to Follow-up (BL-E), and NCCN publishes no distinct stage-IV-resected table.

Sources: jnccn.org · healthhelp.com

Test / examFrequency
Cystoscopyevery 6 months
Cross-sectional imagingas clinically indicated
  • NCCN 2025 follow-up table for bladder-sparing therapy (as summarized in the ASO/ASCO surgical review, Table 5): cystoscopy every 3 months years 1–2, then every 6 months from year 3 onward (including beyond year 5); cytology every 6–12 months years 1–2, then as clinically indicated; CT/MRI abdomen and CT chest every 3–6 months years 1–2. Annual cross-sectional imaging in years 3–5 follows the NCCN follow-up table as summarized from NCCN v3.2023.
  • Biopsy any suspicious lesion seen at cystoscopy; invasive recurrence prompts salvage cystectomy evaluation.
  • FDG PET/CT (category 2B) only when metastatic disease is suspected.

Stage IV metastatic — monitoring on systemic therapy / observation (BL-E Table 7)

Ongoing monitoring — NCCN table has no year-by-year taper or stop point

Test / examFrequency
CT chest/abdomen/pelvisevery 3–6 months, and with any clinical change or new symptoms
CT urography or MR urography (upper tracts + abdomen/pelvis) — alternative to CT chest/abdomen/pelvis aboveevery 3–6 months if clinically indicated, and with any clinical change or new symptoms
FDG-PET/CT (category 2B) — alternative imaging optionas clinically indicated
CBC, CMPevery 1–3 months
Vitamin B12 level — only for patients who have undergone cystectomyannually, based on clinical judgment
Cystoscopyas clinically indicated
Urine cytologyas clinically indicated
  • MONITORING, not post-treatment surveillance: this is NCCN Bladder Cancer BL-E Table 7, 'Metastatic Disease: Surveillance' (BL-E 6 of 6) — v1.2026 Table 7; identical cadence in v5.2024 Table 8.
  • Applies both on systemic therapy and during observation off therapy: BL-A (Principles of Imaging) states 'Metastatic Disease — Patients Being Observed: See Follow-Up (BL-E 6 of 6)'. There is no separate NCCN table for after chemotherapy or after switch-maintenance avelumab completes.
  • BL-9 (stage IVA) and BL-10 (stage IVB) both route metastatic patients to Follow-up (BL-E). No taper and no stop point are specified; BL-E preamble: modify by sites/biology of disease and length of time on treatment, reassess with any new or worsening symptoms regardless of interval, and optimal follow-up duration is undefined.
  • Patients rendered NED by consolidative cystectomy or chemoradiation do NOT use this variant — they follow the post-cystectomy (BL-E Table 5) or bladder-preservation (Table 6) schedule; NCCN publishes no distinct resected-stage-IV table.
  • Imaging cadence corroborated by Cancer Therapy Advisor's NCCN restatement ('Surveillance During Chemo/Immunotherapy': CTU/MRU and CT chest every 3–6 months, CBC/CMP/LFT every 1–3 months) and by Korean J Radiol 2025 (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis every 3–6 months for metastatic disease, with any clinical change or new symptoms).
