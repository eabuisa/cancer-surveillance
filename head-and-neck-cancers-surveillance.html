Head & Neck Cancers — Surveillance & Follow-Up Schedule (NCCN)

Follow-up schedules for head & neck cancers based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

  • Exam schedule and baseline-imaging recommendation per NCCN Head and Neck Cancers follow-up recommendations (JNCCN, unchanged in current versions as restated in the University of Iowa follow-up protocol); follow-up is based on risk of relapse, second primaries, and treatment sequelae. Most recurrences are reported by the patient — symptom education matters.
  • Baseline imaging timing: re-image the primary (and neck, if treated) within 6 months of treatment; payer summaries of NCCN Head and Neck Cancers v2.2024/v4.2024 specify CT/MRI within 3–4 months post-operatively for a new baseline and FDG PET/CT within 3–6 months of definitive radiation or systemic therapy/RT.
  • Routine surveillance imaging beyond the baseline study is NOT recommended in asymptomatic patients — reimage as indicated by signs/symptoms; if the 3-month post-treatment FDG PET/CT is negative, there are no data supporting further routine imaging in an asymptomatic patient with a negative exam (NCCN). Long-term imaging (US, CT, MRI, PET/CT) is tailored, with no consensus type, frequency, or duration.
  • Neck ultrasound: an accepted surveillance modality for the treated neck and for targeted sampling of suspicious nodes, but NCCN sets no fixed interval — tailor to examination findings and accessibility of the site on exam.
  • TSH every 6–12 months if the neck was irradiated (hypothyroidism surveillance) — continues indefinitely.
  • Chest imaging as clinically indicated for patients with a smoking history (see NCCN Lung Cancer Screening).
  • Speech/hearing and swallowing evaluation and rehabilitation, dental evaluation/care, and smoking/alcohol cessation counseling as clinically indicated.
  • HPV-related disease: HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer follows the SAME examination-based surveillance schedule — there is no separate NCCN surveillance pathway. Circulating tumor HPV DNA testing is investigational and is not an NCCN-recommended surveillance standard.
  • Nasopharynx / EBV: for nasopharyngeal carcinoma, plasma EBV DNA is the key adjunct biomarker — NCCN and ESMO recommend evaluating EBV DNA at least annually after treatment (per 2025 reviews of targeted surveillance); a detectable/rising level should prompt imaging. This is in addition to, not instead of, the examination schedule and nasopharyngoscopy.
  • After radiation including the skull base, consider annual screening for hypopituitarism (institutional practice, University of Iowa protocol).

Sources: iowaprotocols.medicine.uiowa.edu · www.healthhelp.com · www.academia.edu

No local therapy (no surgery, no radiation)

  • NCCN head and neck follow-up is keyed entirely to definitive treatment, and there are no consensus guidelines for surveillance imaging type, frequency, or duration. The recurrent/metastatic systemic-therapy pathway states no response-assessment interval.
