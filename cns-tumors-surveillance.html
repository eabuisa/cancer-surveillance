Recurrent / progressive grade 2–3 disease

Test / examFrequency

Circumscribed glioma — after treatment

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast) — circumscribed gliomaevery 3–6 months for 3–5 years, then at least annually as clinically indicated
  • Intervals are from the current NCCN CNS Cancers imaging surveillance table (BRAIN-A, Version 2.2026), which stratifies by histology and treatment received rather than a single interval for all low-grade gliomas.
  • Earlier NCCN versions summarized low-grade glioma surveillance more simply as MRI every 3–6 months for 5 years, then at least annually as clinically indicated; the current treatment-specific table supersedes that summary.
  • New or worsening neurologic symptoms, change in seizures, or increased steroid requirement: obtain MRI as early as possible rather than waiting for the scheduled interval.
  • NCCN emphasizes provider flexibility to adjust imaging frequency to the individual patient and clinical condition.

Sources: www.healthhelp.com · info.nanthealth.com

High-grade glioma (incl. glioblastoma), after treatment

After radiation therapy

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast) — baseline post-RT scanonce, 2–8 weeks after RT

Years 1–3

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 2–4 months

After year 3

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 3–6 months, indefinitely
  • General high-grade glioma schedule per NCCN CNS Cancers (GLIO-11): MRI 2–8 weeks after RT, then every 2–4 months for 3 years, then every 3–6 months indefinitely.
  • CONFLICT / refinement: the current NCCN BRAIN-A table (Version 2.2026) gives glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype its own, more intensive schedule — baseline MRI 3–6 weeks after RT, then every 2–3 months for 3 years, then every 2–4 months indefinitely. Use the GBM-specific intervals for IDH-wildtype glioblastoma.
  • Interpret early post-chemoradiation MRI changes cautiously: pseudoprogression is common in the first 3–6 months after RT + temozolomide.
  • New or worsening neurologic signs/symptoms, change in seizures, or rising steroid requirement: MRI as early as possible.
  • No routine labs or body imaging are recommended for glioma surveillance; contrast MRI of the brain is the surveillance modality.

Sources: Unitedhealthcareonline.com · www.healthhelp.com

Primary CNS lymphoma, after treatment

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 2–3 months

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 6 months

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)annually, indefinitely
  • Per NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers (BRAIN-A): Version 2.2026 explicitly states brain MRI every 2–3 months until year 2. The taper after year 2 (every 6 months to year 5, then annually indefinitely) is as restated in current NCCN-based imaging policies (HealthHelp 2026; Eviti/NantHealth 2026), which phrase the first phase as every 3 months — minor wording difference, flagged not hidden.
  • For patients with previous spine disease, concurrent spine imaging and CSF sampling as clinically indicated.
  • Relapse can occur 10 or more years after successful treatment, which is why follow-up imaging continues annually indefinitely rather than stopping at year 5.

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · www.healthhelp.com · info.nanthealth.com

Leptomeningeal metastases, after treatment

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain and/or total spine (site of involvement, with contrast)every 2–3 months

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain and/or total spine (site of involvement, with contrast)every 6 months

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain and/or total spine (site of involvement, with contrast)annually, indefinitely
  • Per NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers, Version 2.2026 (BRAIN-A, leptomeningeal metastases): baseline staging is MRI of the entire neuroaxis (brain and total spine); follow-up imaging targets the site(s) of involvement.
  • Follow-up pairs neuroimaging with neurologic assessment and CSF cytology/flow cytometry as clinically indicated for response assessment; NCCN sets no fixed CSF sampling interval.

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · www.healthhelp.com

Brain metastases (limited or extensive), after treatment

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 2–3 months

After year 2

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 4–6 months, indefinitely
  • Per NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers surveillance (limited and extensive brain metastases pathways), as restated in current NCCN-based imaging policies (HealthHelp 2026) and quoted in the 2024 stereotactic radiosurgery guideline review from NCCN Version 1.2024. Applies regardless of primary tumor type or lesion number, after SRS, whole-brain RT, and/or resection.
  • Minor version difference flagged: the 2023 restatement phrased the intensive phase as a full 2 years; current restatements say 1–2 years, as shown.

Sources: www.healthhelp.com · www.e-neurofunction.org

Medulloblastoma (adult), after treatment

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 2–3 months
MRI spine (with contrast)as clinically indicated (prior spine disease or new/progressing symptoms)

Years 3–10

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 6–12 months

After year 10

Test / examFrequency
MRI brain (with contrast)every 1–2 years, or as clinically indicated
  • Per NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers, Version 2.2026 (BRAIN-A, adult medulloblastoma); the HealthHelp 2026 NCCN-based restatement concurs (brain MRI every 2–3 months for 2 years, then every 6–12 months through years 5–10, then every 1–2 years). Pediatric medulloblastoma follows the pediatric CNS schedules, not this one.
