  • NCCN Hepatocellular Carcinoma guideline schedule as restated in both sources: imaging and AFP every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6 months; surveillance imaging and AFP should continue for at least 5 years, and thereafter screening depends on HCC risk factors. One source cites the NCCN guideline as published March 2025; the same schedule appears in NCCN HCC Version 3.2024.
  • After locoregional therapy (including ablation), early imaging may be considered per local protocol, in addition to the schedule above.
  • Conflict flagged: American Cancer Society patient guidance simplifies the post-2-year interval to imaging and blood tests every 6–12 months; the NCCN clinician guideline value (every 6 months) is used here.
  • Ultrasound is the NCCN screening modality for at-risk patients without treated HCC; it is not the modality specified for post-resection/ablation surveillance, which specifies multiphasic CT or MRI. No routine PET/CT is included in the NCCN surveillance schedule.

Sources: emedicine.medscape.com · www.rarediseaseadvisor.com · www.cancer.org

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
Imaging and AFP for late recurrenceas clinically indicated; no consensus schedule
  • Data are thin and there is no consensus post-transplant schedule: this variant is intentionally brief. NCCN groups transplant with resection and locoregional therapy under its general HCC surveillance schedule (imaging and AFP every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6 months, for at least 5 years), but it has no separate transplant-specific table.
  • The intervals above instead reflect RETREAT-score–stratified consensus practice restated in the transplant reviews: RETREAT 1–3, imaging + AFP every 6 months for the first 2 years; RETREAT 4, every 6 months for 5 years; RETREAT ≥5, every 3–4 months for the first 2 years, then every 6 months up to year 5. RETREAT 0 patients have very low recurrence risk and some centers offer no or minimal surveillance — a direct conflict with applying the blanket NCCN schedule to all recipients.
  • AASLD 2023 guidance recommends surveillance for post-transplant recurrence with multiphasic contrast-enhanced abdominal CT or MRI and chest CT, but states the optimal timing and duration are not established.
  • Most recurrences occur within the first 2–3 years and are often extrahepatic (hence chest CT); routine bone and pelvis imaging is not commonly performed.

Sources: emedicine.medscape.com · www.mdpi.com · www.eujtransplantation.com

Imaging — multiphasic abdomen MRI or multiphase CT for liver assessment, CT chest, and CT/MRI pelvisevery 6 months
AFPevery 6 months
  • NCCN HCC v1.2025, HCC-5 (Liver-confined, Unresectable, and Deemed Ineligible for Transplant): the page's single Surveillance column applies to every treatment listed on that page — locoregional therapy, systemic therapy, clinical trial, or best supportive care — so it covers patients treated with systemic therapy alone who never received local therapy. Surveillance imaging and AFP should continue for at least 5 years; thereafter screening depends on HCC risk factors (footnote jj). Same cadence restated for NCCN HCC v2.2025 (Medscape).
  • Does NOT cover extrahepatic/metastatic HCC: HCC-6 carries a Response Assessment column only ('Assess for response') with no interval stated, and the Discussion (MS-40) recommends only 'periodic' response assessment with cross-sectional imaging for advanced HCC on systemic therapy — no NCCN cadence exists for that subgroup.
  • On HCC-5, 'consider early imaging per local protocol' is specified for locoregional therapy only, not for systemic-therapy-only patients.
