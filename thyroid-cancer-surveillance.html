Long term

Test / examFrequency
Monitoring discontinuationafter 10–15 years without recurrence, no further thyroid-cancer monitoring is indicated for low-risk patients
  • REPLACEMENT — corrected against NCCN Thyroid Carcinoma v2.2026 (PAP-7/FOLL-6/ONC-6). The previous version's 'neck ultrasound every 6 months for 2 years, then annually' came from payer summaries of v1.2024 that conflated the ACTIVE SURVEILLANCE cadence for unresected low-risk papillary cancer (THYR-D) with post-treatment monitoring. Current NCCN post-treatment monitoring for the lobectomy and total-thyroidectomy-without-RAI pathways is: physical exam, TSH, Tg/TgAb, and neck ultrasound at 6–12 months; then Tg/TgAb annually if stable (total thyroidectomy) and neck ultrasound as clinically indicated.
  • Version drift to be aware of: NCCN v2.2024 worded post-lobectomy/no-RAI ultrasound as 'annually, then as clinically indicated every 3–5 years if stable,' and older payer summaries still circulating list annual ultrasounds — v2.2026 wording ('as clinically indicated' after the 6–12-month scan) is shown here.
  • After lobectomy the remaining lobe produces thyroglobulin, so Tg is not useful for trending (NCCN: trending Tg is not recommended after lobectomy); surveillance rests on examination, TSH, and ultrasound, with a one-time Tg/TgAb measurement that may be considered.
  • TSH goal in low-risk disease-free patients is the low-normal/normal range — suppression is not required (Principles of TSH Suppression, THYR-A).
  • Routine diagnostic RAI whole-body scanning is NOT recommended in low-risk patients with no evidence of disease.
  • Rising Tg or new TgAb → additional imaging (neck CT/MRI with contrast or chest CT, FDG-PET, or RAI imaging) and biopsy of suspicious areas (consider Tg washout).

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · thyca.org

Known iodine-avid disease

Test / examFrequency
Follow-up cross-sectional imaging (CT or MRI) of known iodine-avid diseaseas indicated to monitor known disease
  • REPLACEMENT — corrected against NCCN Thyroid Carcinoma v2.2026 (PAP-8). Version 1.2026 changed neck ultrasound in no-evidence-of-disease patients after total thyroidectomy + RAI from 'annually for 5 years, then less often' to 'every 1–3 years for 5–8 years, and then discontinue.' The previous version of this variant still showed the retired annual schedule in its bands (its note flagged the change); the bands now match current NCCN. The 2025 ATA guideline independently adopted the same 1–3-year spacing for 5–8 years, then discontinuation, in excellent responders.
  • Rising Tg or new TgAb → additional imaging (neck CT/MRI with contrast or chest/abdomen CT, FDG-PET, or RAI imaging); biopsy of suspicious areas (consider Tg washout).
  • TSH suppression is risk-based (THYR-A): <0.1 mU/L with structural residual or high-risk disease; 0.1–0.5 mU/L for biochemical-only (Tg-positive, imaging-negative) disease; normal range once disease-free for several years.
  • The 10–15-year 'no further monitoring' rule is stated by NCCN for LOW-risk patients; it is not stated for this higher-risk pathway.

Test / examFrequency

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
Physical examinationevery 6 months
Calcitonin & CEA (if post-op values normal)every 6–12 months
Neck ultrasound (central & lateral compartments)consider annually if markers undetectable; obtain if markers detectable
FDG-PET/CT or Ga-68 DOTATATE PET/CT, or MRI with contrast of neck, chest, and abdomen with liver protocolas indicated, based on calcitonin/CEA doubling time (if calcitonin detectable or CEA elevated)

After year 2

Test / examFrequency
Physical examinationannually
Calcitonin & CEAannually if stable/undetectable; every 6–12 months if detectable (see note)
Neck ultrasoundas clinically indicated by marker levels
  • Disease monitoring per NCCN Thyroid Carcinoma, medullary pathway: basal calcitonin and CEA are first checked 2–3 months post-operatively. If basal calcitonin is undetectable and CEA is normal — annual calcitonin/CEA, with neck ultrasound (central and lateral compartments) to be considered. If calcitonin is detectable or CEA elevated — calcitonin/CEA every 6–12 months, neck ultrasound, and additional studies or more frequent testing based on calcitonin/CEA doubling time.
  • If calcitonin is ≥150 pg/mL, obtain cross-sectional imaging (contrast CT or MRI of neck, chest, and liver/abdomen; bone scan or spinal MRI as indicated) in addition to neck ultrasound.
  • No additional imaging is required while calcitonin and CEA remain stable.
  • For MEN2A/MEN2B patients: annual biochemical screening for pheochromocytoma, and for hyperparathyroidism in MEN2A (NCCN).
  • Thyroglobulin plays no role in medullary surveillance (C-cell tumor); TSH is followed only for levothyroxine replacement after total thyroidectomy.

Stage IVC (metastatic) — disease monitoring and management

Test / examFrequency
Imaging (CT or MRI with contrast) of brain, neck, chest, abdomen, and pelvisat frequent intervals as clinically indicated
FDG-PET/CTconsider once, at 3–6 months after initial therapy
Disease monitoring if no evidence of diseasecontinued (ANAP-3)
  • Per NCCN Thyroid Carcinoma v2.2026, Anaplastic Carcinoma pathway (ANAP-2/ANAP-3): incomplete (R2) resection of stage IVA/IVB disease routes to 'Disease Monitoring and Management' (ANAP-3), the same page used for stage IVC. NCCN does NOT state a numeric surveillance interval for ATC — the algorithm language is 'imaging (CT or MRI with contrast) of brain, neck, chest, abdomen, and pelvis at frequent intervals as clinically indicated,' with FDG-PET/CT to be considered 3–6 months after initial therapy and continued disease monitoring if NED. HealthHelp's 2026 imaging policy restates this NCCN language verbatim.
  • CONFLICT / OPERATIONALIZATION FLAGGED: payer restatements convert 'frequent intervals' into numbers — eviCore (ONC-6, citing NCCN) describes close monitoring with CT neck/chest/abdomen/pelvis and MRI brain every 3 months for 2 years (most relapses occur in that window) and CT every 2 cycles while on systemic therapy. These numeric intervals are payer operationalizations, NOT NCCN algorithm text, and are recorded here so they are not mistaken for an NCCN-stated cadence.
  • Anaplastic carcinoma is the most aggressive thyroid cancer (>50% present with distant metastases; 5-year survival <10%), so surveillance is individualized and typically intertwined with systemic-therapy response assessment, palliative care, and clinical-trial follow-up.
  • Thyroglobulin plays no role in ATC surveillance; TSH is followed only for levothyroxine replacement after total thyroidectomy.

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · www.healthhelp.com · www.evicore.com · info.nanthealth.com

Medullary — metastatic disease monitoring, incl. on systemic therapy (on-treatment monitoring)

Ongoing, while metastatic disease is monitored

Test / examFrequency
Serum calcitonin & CEAevery 6–12 months; more frequent testing if significantly rising, based on calcitonin/CEA doubling time
Cross-sectional imaging (CT or MRI of neck, chest, and abdomen/liver; bone scan or whole-body MRI in select patients)as indicated — obtain when calcitonin is ≥150 pg/mL, markers are rising, or symptoms develop; NCCN states no fixed imaging interval
Neck ultrasoundas indicated for locoregional assessment
FDG-PET/CT or Ga-68 DOTATATE PET/CT, or MRI with contrast of neck, chest, and abdomen with liver protocolas indicated, based on calcitonin/CEA doubling time
  • Stage IV unit per NCCN Thyroid Carcinoma v2.2026, medullary pathway: for distant metastases that are asymptomatic or stable, 'disease monitoring' is an explicit management option alongside local therapy and systemic therapy (MEDU-7); monitoring follows the disease-monitoring schedule used for persistent disease (MEDU-5 and Discussion): serum calcitonin and CEA every 6–12 months when detectable, with additional studies or more frequent testing based on calcitonin/CEA doubling times. The same marker cadence continues for patients receiving systemic therapy (vandetanib, cabozantinib, or a RET inhibitor).
  • Rising calcitonin or CEA alone, without structural progression, is NOT an indication to start systemic therapy (NCCN medullary footnote and Discussion: outside clinical trials, no therapeutic intervention is recommended on the basis of abnormal markers alone). Systemic therapy is directed at disease that is symptomatic or progressing by RECIST.
  • No fixed restaging interval on kinase inhibitors: NCCN states no numeric imaging cadence for metastatic medullary cancer on systemic therapy; imaging is triggered by marker level (≥150 pg/mL), doubling time, rising markers, or symptoms. This variant is the stage IV framing of the same monitoring carried post-operatively in the 'Medullary' variant, broken out because NCCN lists disease monitoring as a distinct management pathway for distant metastases.
  • Corroboration: the Medscape thyroid cancer treatment protocol restating the NCCN medullary pathway gives the same monitoring — serum calcitonin and CEA every 6 to 12 months, more frequent testing based on calcitonin/CEA doubling time, and no additional imaging required while calcitonin and CEA are stable.

Sources: dr2pp.oss.ns-svc.cn · emedicine.medscape.com

Differentiated — never operated: active surveillance of low-risk papillary carcinoma (no local therapy)

Years 1–2 of active surveillance

Test / examFrequency
Neck ultrasound (thyroid and lymph node regions)every 6 months

After year 2, if stable

Test / examFrequency
Neck ultrasound (thyroid and lymph node regions)annually, if stable
  • Applies only to very low-risk papillary carcinoma managed by active surveillance — typically a small (≤1 cm) cancer confined to the thyroid, with no lymph-node involvement, extrathyroidal extension, or aggressive features (NCCN Principles of Active Surveillance, THYR-D, v2.2026). Changes on ultrasound (growth or new nodal disease) prompt discussion of surgery.
  • Thyroglobulin is not a surveillance marker in this setting: the thyroid gland is still in situ, so Tg reflects normal thyroid tissue. Surveillance rests on ultrasound.
  • This variant does NOT cover unresectable/metastatic differentiated thyroid cancer on systemic therapy (e.g. lenvatinib): NCCN states no monitoring cadence for that scenario — the DTC monitoring pages (PAP-7) are post-thyroidectomy — and no variant is drafted for it. Medullary metastatic disease is already covered by the existing 'medullary-metastatic' variant (calcitonin/CEA every 6–12 months).
  • Interval quoted identically by NCCN Guidelines for Patients, Thyroid Cancer 2026 (based on NCCN Thyroid Carcinoma v2.2026) and by Eviti's NCCN-referenced thyroid imaging guideline (2026), corroborated by eviCore ONC-6.

Sources: WWW.NCCN.ORG · info.nanthealth.com · www.evicore.com
