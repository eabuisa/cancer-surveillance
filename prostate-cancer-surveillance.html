Years 1–5

Test / examFrequency
Serum PSAevery 6–12 months
Digital rectal exam (DRE)annually

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
Serum PSAannually
Digital rectal exam (DRE)annually
  • NCCN Prostate Cancer (v1.2025, as restated in payer policies and reviews): PSA every 6–12 months for the first 5 years, then annually after definitive therapy.
  • For patients at high risk of recurrence, PSA every 3 months may be preferred (NCCN).
  • Biochemical recurrence after RP = PSA ≥0.2 ng/mL confirmed by a second measurement (AUA/NCCN).
  • Annual DRE is retained because local recurrence can occur in the absence of a PSA rise.
  • No routine imaging in asymptomatic patients with undetectable PSA.

Sources: www.capbluecross.com · www.avalonhcs.com · mdpi-res.com

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
Serum PSAannually
Digital rectal exam (DRE)annually
  • NCCN monitoring after RT: PSA every 6–12 months for 5 years, then annually; several NCCN summaries specify PSA every 6 months for the first 5 years after RT. DRE annually; the clinician may omit DRE if the PSA is undetectable (per NCCN as restated in the Avalon policy).
  • Biochemical failure after RT = PSA rise ≥2 ng/mL above nadir (Phoenix definition). PSA may take 18–36 months to reach nadir; a transient PSA 'bounce' is common and benign.
  • No routine imaging in asymptomatic patients without a rising PSA.

Sources: www.avalonhcs.com · www.capbluecross.com · mdpi-res.com

Advanced disease — monitoring on ADT / systemic therapy

Ongoing, while on ADT / systemic therapy

Test / examFrequency
Physical examination + PSAEvery 3–6 months
Serum testosterone — document castrate level (<50 ng/dL)Consider monitoring 12 weeks after first dose of LHRH therapy, then upon increase in PSA; document castrate level if clinically indicated, and if progression occurs on ADT (CRPC)
Bone scanFor symptoms, and as often as every 6–12 months to monitor ADT
Soft-tissue imaging (CT or MRI) — may be obtained regularly during systemic therapy to assess clinical benefitPeriodic imaging to monitor treatment response; also for symptoms or increasing PSA (no fixed interval stated by NCCN)
Imaging for workup of progression (bone and soft-tissue evaluation; PSMA PET may be considered as an alternative to CT/MRI/bone scan)As clinically indicated
  • Monitoring during therapy, NOT post-treatment surveillance.
  • Same monitoring wording applies to mCSPC on ADT ± ARPI (PROS-13/14/15) and, via footnote, to M0 CRPC monitoring (PROS-16); PROS-17/18 (M1 CRPC) state no separate PSA or imaging interval.
  • Bone-scan cadence is from Principles of Imaging (PROS-E), not the treatment algorithms, which say only 'periodic imaging.' PROS-E states the need for routine soft-tissue imaging 'remains unclear' — no soft-tissue interval is stated.
  • PROS-E also states 8- to 12-week imaging intervals 'appear reasonable' in CRPC — single-source, soft wording; reported in the audit as UNDECIDED and deliberately NOT encoded as an item.
  • No PSMA PET monitoring interval exists in NCCN; in M1 CRPC, PSMA PET is positioned only to determine Lu-177-PSMA-617 candidacy and as workup for progression.
  • On intermittent ADT, NCCN requires close monitoring of PSA and testosterone (and possibly imaging), especially during off-treatment periods — no interval stated.

Sources: jnjmedicalcloud.pl · info.nanthealth.com · www.smpamedicalaffairs.com · bighealth.fudan.edu.cn

Oligometastatic CSPC — monitoring after metastasis-directed therapy (MDT ± ADT)

Ongoing, after MDT ± ADT (metachronous oligometastatic CSPC)

Test / examFrequency
Physical examination + PSAEvery 3–6 months
ImagingFor symptoms; consider periodic imaging to monitor treatment response (no interval stated by NCCN)
  • Monitoring on the PROS-13 pathway, NOT post-treatment surveillance — this is the only follow-up wording NCCN gives for definitively treated oligometastatic prostate cancer.
  • Applies to metachronous oligometastatic CSPC treated with MDT ± ADT. Synchronous oligometastatic disease is monitored as M1 CSPC (see advanced-adt).
  • PROS-M (Principles of Metastasis-Directed Therapy) contains no follow-up cadence of its own.
  • NCCN publishes no surveillance table for prostate cancer resected to NED.
