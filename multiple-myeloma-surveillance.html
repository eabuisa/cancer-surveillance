After treatment / maintenance

Ongoing

Test / examFrequency
CBC with differential and platelet countevery 3–6 months
Serum chemistry (creatinine, corrected calcium, albumin)every 3–6 months
Serum quantitative immunoglobulins, SPEP with immunofixation (SIFE if SPEP negative)every 3–6 months; immunofixation only if protein electrophoresis is negative during follow-up
Serum free light chain assayevery 3–6 months
24-hour urine total protein, UPEP with immunofixationevery 3–6 months; as clinically indicated if abnormal at baseline (current versions)
Whole-body imaging (low-dose CT, FDG-PET/CT, or whole-body MRI)annually or as clinically indicated
Bone marrow aspirate and biopsy (± MRD assessment)as clinically indicated
  • Per NCCN Multiple Myeloma follow-up/surveillance (MYEL-4/MYEL-5), Version 3.2024; the panel and 3–6 month interval are retained in Version 5.2026 (Jan 2026), which revised the 24-hour urine studies to 'as needed / as clinically indicated if abnormal at baseline or if FLC levels change significantly'.
  • Relapsed / progressive disease: NCCN specifies no separate monitoring cadence. The relapse pathway (MYEL-6, Version 5.2026) directs follow-up with this same MYEL-4 test list — footnote dd: 'Follow up with the tests listed on MYEL-4 under Follow-up/Surveillance' — so patients on therapy for relapsed/refractory myeloma are monitored with this panel at the same 3–6 month interval; regimen-specific toxicity labs and response checks are set by the treatment protocol, not by a distinct NCCN surveillance schedule.
  • Patients on active maintenance therapy are typically seen and labbed more often per the treatment protocol; the 3–6 month interval is the NCCN surveillance floor for follow-up after primary therapy.
  • MRD testing after therapy: consider as indicated for prognostication, after shared decision-making with the patient.
  • Plain skeletal survey is not the preferred surveillance modality when whole-body low-dose CT, PET/CT, or MRI is available.

Test / examFrequency

Bone marrow aspirate and biopsyas clinically indicated (suspected progression)
  • Per NCCN Multiple Myeloma Version 5.2026 (January 9, 2026), MYEL-3 'Smoldering Myeloma (Asymptomatic): Primary Treatment and Follow-up/Surveillance': low-risk — 'Observe at 3- to 6-month intervals (category 1)'; high-risk — 'Observe at 3-month intervals', with clinical trial preferred and, in select patients, daratumumab (category 1) or lenalidomide (category 2B) as alternatives to observation.
  • This variant covers patients who have received NO therapy at all — the observation arm of MYEL-3. It is distinct from the existing 'monitor' variant (MYEL-4/MYEL-5 follow-up after treatment of symptomatic myeloma), which does not address smoldering myeloma.
  • Imaging wording (MYEL-3 / Principles of Imaging, v5.2026): advanced whole-body imaging (FDG-PET/CT, low-dose CT, or MRI without contrast) annually or as clinically indicated; if imaging is the only parameter suggesting progression and findings are doubtful, repeat with the same technique in 3–6 months.
  • MGUS note (not a MYEL-3 table row): ARUP Consult's NCCN-aligned summary gives low-risk MGUS follow-up as SPEP at 6 months, then every 2–3 years if stable, with no routine marrow or imaging unless biochemical progression — included here as a note only because a verbatim NCCN MGUS cadence table could not be verified from two independent sources in this pass.
  • Progression to symptomatic myeloma (CRAB / SLiM criteria on MYEL-A) moves the patient to the symptomatic-myeloma pathways (MYEL-4) and the existing 'monitor' variant.
