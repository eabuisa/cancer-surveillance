  • Per NCCN Merkel Cell Carcinoma v2.2025 (MCC-6): physical exam every 3–6 months for 3 years, then every 6–12 months thereafter; imaging and other studies as clinically indicated, with routine imaging surveillance recommended for patients at high risk of recurrence (immunosuppression, stage II–IV, MCPyV-seronegative status, older age, male sex).
  • MCPyV oncoprotein antibody: obtain baseline within 3 months of treatment. Seronegative patients have a higher recurrence risk and may warrant more intensive surveillance; in seropositive patients a rising titer may precede clinical recurrence.
  • Most (~95%) first recurrences occur within 3 years, but late recurrences occur — indefinite exam follow-up is recommended.
  • Immunocompromised patients are at higher risk of recurrence; more frequent follow-up may be indicated, and immunosuppression should be minimized as clinically feasible.
  • Conflict flagged: an older NCCN version (v1.2018, cited in secondary sources) described skin/nodal exams every 1–3 months in year 1, then every 3–6 months in year 2, then annually. This is superseded by the current every 3–6 months for 3 years, then every 6–12 months schedule (v2.2022 onward, confirmed in v2.2025).
  • NCCN MCC-6 specifies no stop date for AMERK or ctDNA surveillance: the footnotes attach both tests to the entire follow-up block, including the every 6–12 month phase after year 3, and each is often obtained every 3 months during surveillance.

Sources: merkelcell.org · www.frontiersin.org · amp.cancer.org

Metastatic / unresectable — on immunotherapy (no MCC-specific NCCN monitoring cadence)

  • NCCN Merkel Cell Carcinoma (v2.2025) publishes NO MCC-specific restaging or monitoring interval for patients on immunotherapy (avelumab, pembrolizumab, retifanlimab, nivolumab) for unresectable stage III or metastatic stage IV disease. No cadence is shown for this pathway because none exists in NCCN — and no other site's or payer's interval has been imported in its place.
  • What NCCN does state: the metastatic pathway routes back to the MCC-6 follow-up block (see 'After treatment' in this app) — physical examination (complete skin and nodal) every 3–6 months for 3 years, then every 6–12 months; AMERK/ctDNA every 3 months if applicable; imaging 'as clinically indicated' (routine imaging recommended for high-risk patients). NCCN does not state whether that block is intended to govern patients still on therapy, and it specifies no on-treatment imaging interval.
  • Payer figures are not NCCN and are not adopted: eviCore offers only its generic cross-disease phase rules (imaging every 2 cycles on immunotherapy) and a node-positive surveillance figure of its own (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis every 6 months for 5 years) — neither is MCC-6 wording or an MCC-specific on-therapy cadence.

Sources: www.evicore.com · merkelcell.org · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
