  • Conflict flagged — post-year-2 interval: older NCCN restatements (JNCCN/Annals of Surgical Oncology review and a 2025 case-report restatement) say surveillance every 3–6 months for 2 years, then annually thereafter. More recent clinician-guideline restatements (NCCN Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma Versions 1.2020/1.2021, Taipei VGH practice guideline based on NCCN 1.2022, and Evolent 2025 imaging guidelines citing NCCN) say then every 6–12 months as clinically indicated. The latter, more current phrasing is used here; there is no guideline-defined stop at 5 years in these sources.
  • Imaging anatomy has evolved: older sources specify abdominal (or abdomen/pelvis) CT only, whereas the current NCCN Guidelines for Patients (Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma Version 2.2026 — 22 Apr 2026) list surveillance tests as H&P for symptom assessment, CA 19-9, CT scan of the chest, and CT or MRI scan of the abdomen and pelvis with contrast — the items above reflect that current list, at the clinician-guideline interval.
  • CA 19-9 and imaging surveillance are category 2B (non-uniform consensus) in NCCN, based on expert opinion with limited data that earlier detection improves outcomes; H&P for symptom assessment is the category 2A item. Surveillance is intended for patients with no evidence of recurrence or metastatic disease after resection ± adjuvant therapy.
  • No routine PET/CT surveillance schedule is stated in the NCCN-based sources used here.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma Version 2.2026 (patient guideline, test list only — it states visits 'as needed' and gives no interval), Versions 1.2020/1.2021 clinician guideline excerpts (interval), and Version 2.2017 JNCCN (interval, historical).
  • CA 19-9 elevation, without other evidence of disease recurrence, is not a clear indication for treatment (NCCN Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma Version 2.2025, PANC-8 footnote ii).
