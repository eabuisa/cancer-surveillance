Gastric Cancer — Surveillance & Follow-Up Schedule (NCCN)

Follow-up schedules for gastric cancer based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

Stage I after resection

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalevery 3–6 months for 1–2 years
CBC and chemistry profileas clinically indicated
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastas clinically indicated, based on symptoms and concern for recurrence
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)as clinically indicated after surgical resection
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 and iron (also zinc, calcium, vitamin D per survivorship guidance)monitor and treat as indicated; especially after total gastrectomy
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 levelat least every 6 months, if not on parenteral B12 supplement
Nutrition monitoring — CBC and iron levelsat least annually
H. pylori testingtest once and eradicate if positive (patients with H. pylori infection after subtotal resection)

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalevery 6–12 months for 3–5 years
CBC and chemistry profileas clinically indicated
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastas clinically indicated
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)as clinically indicated after surgical resection
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 and ironmonitor and treat as indicated; lifelong monitoring after gastrectomy
Bone density testingconsider at 3 years post-gastrectomy (and in patients who are post-menopausal or >50 years of age)

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalas clinically indicated; routine gastric cancer-specific surveillance not recommended beyond 5 years
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastnot routinely recommended beyond 5 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)not routinely recommended beyond 5 years; as clinically indicated
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 and ironlifelong monitoring after gastrectomy, as clinically indicated
  • Pathologic stage I here is pT1a/T1b N0 treated by surgical resection, per NCCN GAST-7 as restated in both sources. No routine scheduled CT or EGD for surgically resected stage I — both are as clinically indicated, unlike stage II–III.
  • Different sub-case within stage I: if stage I was treated by endoscopic resection (ER) rather than surgery, EGD is scheduled — every 6 months for 1 year, then annually for up to 5 years, thereafter as needed based on symptoms and/or radiographic findings. This ER schedule does not apply to the surgical-resection variant above.
  • For total gastrectomy, endoscopy has no routine surveillance role unless the patient is symptomatic (NCCN Gastric Version 3.2025, GAST-7 footnote); follow the other surveillance items as above.
  • Nutrition: NCCN survivorship guidance calls for lifelong follow-up for nutritional sequelae of gastrectomy (vitamin B12, iron, zinc, calcium, vitamin D) and consideration of routine supplementation; the GAST-7 surveillance table itself phrases B12/iron as monitor and treat as indicated, with no fixed interval — no fixed B12 interval is stated in the surveillance table used here.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026 — 21 Jan 2026 (NCCN Guidelines for Patients, Stomach Cancer 2026, the primary source for the frequencies above) and the NCCN version restated in the Medscape gastric cancer review (ref. 24 in that article).

Sources: Www.Nccn.Org · www.staging.medscape.com

Stage II–III after resection

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalevery 3–6 months for 1–2 years
CBC and chemistry profileas clinically indicated
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrast (preferred)every 6 months for the first 2 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)as clinically indicated after partial or subtotal gastrectomy
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 and iron (also zinc, calcium, vitamin D per survivorship guidance)monitor and treat as indicated; especially after total gastrectomy
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 levelat least every 6 months, if not on parenteral B12 supplement
Nutrition monitoring — CBC and iron levelsat least annually
H. pylori testingtest once and eradicate if positive (patients with H. pylori infection after subtotal resection)

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalevery 6–12 months for 3–5 years
CBC and chemistry profileas clinically indicated
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrast (preferred)annually, up to 5 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)as clinically indicated after partial or subtotal gastrectomy
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 and ironmonitor and treat as indicated; lifelong monitoring after gastrectomy
Bone density testingconsider at 3 years post-gastrectomy (and in patients who are post-menopausal or >50 years of age)

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalas clinically indicated; routine gastric cancer-specific surveillance not recommended beyond 5 years
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastnot routinely recommended beyond 5 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)not routinely recommended beyond 5 years; as clinically indicated
Nutrition monitoring — vitamin B12 and ironlifelong monitoring after gastrectomy, as clinically indicated
  • NCCN category includes pathologic stage II/III and yp stage I–III treated with neoadjuvant ± adjuvant therapy (GAST-7); the patient-guideline wording is pathologic stage 2 or 3, or pathologic stage 1–3 treated with systemic therapy before/after surgery, with the same CT schedule.
  • For total gastrectomy, endoscopy has no routine surveillance role unless the patient is symptomatic (NCCN Gastric Version 3.2025, GAST-7 footnote) — the EGD item above applies to partial/subtotal gastrectomy, as clinically indicated.
  • If CT cannot be performed, alternative imaging such as PET/CT or MRI may be used as clinically indicated (NCCN GAST-7 footnote) — routine PET/CT is not the scheduled surveillance modality; CT is preferred.
  • Nutrition: NCCN survivorship guidance calls for lifelong follow-up for nutritional sequelae of gastrectomy (vitamin B12, iron, zinc, calcium, vitamin D); the surveillance table phrases B12/iron as monitor and treat as indicated, with no fixed interval.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026 — 21 Jan 2026 (NCCN Guidelines for Patients, Stomach Cancer 2026) and the NCCN version restated in the Medscape gastric cancer review; a peer-reviewed recurrence-hazard review independently restates the same stage II/III CT schedule (every 6 months for 2 years, then annually up to 5 years).

Sources: Www.Nccn.Org · www.staging.medscape.com · www.springermedizin.de

Early cancer (Tis/T1a) — after endoscopic resection (ESD/EMR)

Years 1–2

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalevery 3–6 months for 1–2 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)every 6 months for 1 year, then annually (duration differs for Tis vs T1a — see notes)
CBC and chemistry profileas clinically indicated
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastas clinically indicated, based on symptoms and concern for recurrence — not routine
H. pylori testingtest once and eradicate if positive (NCCN recommendation for all early gastric cancer — see notes)

Years 3–5

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalevery 6–12 months for 3–5 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)annually (Tis: annually for 3 years after year 1; T1a: annually for up to 5 years)
CBC and chemistry profileas clinically indicated
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastas clinically indicated — not routine

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physicalas clinically indicated; routine gastric cancer-specific surveillance not recommended beyond 5 years
Upper GI endoscopy (EGD)not routinely recommended beyond 5 years (T1a annual EGD runs up to 5 years)
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrastnot routinely recommended beyond 5 years; as clinically indicated
  • Per NCCN GAST-7 (Follow-up/Surveillance), the history & physical cadence is shared by all gastric follow-up rows; the EGD schedule is what distinguishes endoscopically resected early cancer from surgically resected stage I (where EGD is only as clinically indicated).
  • CONFLICT / version nuance on the annual EGD phase: GAST-7 phrases the post-year-1 annual EGD as 'annually for 3 years' for Tis successfully treated by ER, but 'annually for up to 5 years' for pT1a treated by ER (NCCN Gastric Versions 1.2023–3.2025 as restated in the sources). Both caps are shown rather than merged.
  • H. pylori: NCCN Gastric Version 3.2025 (workup principles, GAST-1A) states 'Test for H. pylori infection and eradicate in all patients with early gastric cancer if positive.' This is a one-time test-and-treat recommendation, not a recurring surveillance interval — GAST-7 states no repeat-testing cadence.
  • No scheduled CT is stated for endoscopically resected early cancer — imaging is as clinically indicated only, unlike the scheduled CT in the stage II–III resection variant.
  • The nutrition-monitoring items (vitamin B12, iron) in the resection variants are gastrectomy-related; GAST-7 states no equivalent item for endoscopically resected patients, whose stomach is intact.
  • NCCN versions cited by sources: Gastric Cancer Version 1.2025 (HealthHelp imaging guideline restating NCCN surveillance) and Version 3.2025 (GAST-7 follow-up/surveillance table, guideline text).

Sources: www.healthhelp.com · hsnow.net · bookcafe.yuntsg.com
