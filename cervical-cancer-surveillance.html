After year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical exam (incl. pelvic exam)annually, based on risk of recurrence
  • Surveillance per NCCN Cervical Cancer (CERV-10); the same schedule is restated in the 2026 Society of Gynecologic Oncology (SGO) clinical practice statement on post-treatment surveillance.
  • Visit interval is individualized by recurrence risk — high-risk patients are often seen every 3 months during the first 2 years.
  • Routine surveillance imaging is NOT recommended in asymptomatic patients; imaging (CXR, CT, PET/CT, MRI) only as indicated by symptoms or examination findings. Suspected recurrence should be biopsy-proven.
  • Cytology can be considered to detect lower genital tract dysplasia, but its value in detecting recurrent cervical cancer is limited and the yield for asymptomatic recurrence is low; prior radiation raises the false-positive rate.
  • Educate on symptoms of recurrence (vaginal bleeding/discharge, pelvic or leg pain, lymphedema, weight loss); most recurrences are symptom-detected.

Sources: conferences.iaea.org · www.sciencedirect.com · saskcancer.ca
