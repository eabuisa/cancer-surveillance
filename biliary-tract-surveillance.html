  • NCCN Biliary Tract Cancers Version 1.2026 (March 10, 2026), GALL-6: 'consider imaging every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6–12 months for up to 5 years, or as clinically indicated' and 'consider CEA and CA 19-9 as clinically indicated.' NCCN footnote: there are no data to support a specific surveillance schedule or tests; the schedule is based on the surveillance used in the phase III BILCAP trial.
  • The NCCN clinician table for gallbladder says only 'consider imaging' without naming a modality; the multiphasic CT/MRI of chest and abdomen/pelvis specification comes from the NCCN Guidelines for Patients (based on Version 1.2026), which describes surveillance as CA 19-9 and CEA testing plus multiphasic CT/MRI of the chest and abdomen/pelvis at the same intervals.
  • CA 19-9 is not a fixed-interval item in NCCN: it is 'as clinically indicated' only. No routine PET/CT is included in the NCCN surveillance schedule.

Sources: www.NCCN.org · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · www.anthem.com

Test / examFrequency
Multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with IV contrastevery 6–12 months (up to 5 years)

CEA and CA 19-9as clinically indicated

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
Multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with IV contrast and chest CT ± contrastas clinically indicated
CEA and CA 19-9as clinically indicated
  • Applies to both intrahepatic and extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma after resection. NCCN Biliary Tract Cancers Version 1.2026 (INTRA-2 and EXTRA-2; also restated from Version 2.2025): 'consider multiphasic abdomen/pelvis CT/MRI with IV contrast and chest CT ± contrast every 3–6 months for 2 years, then every 6–12 months for up to 5 years, or as clinically indicated.'
  • NCCN wording is 'consider' — one secondary source notes explicitly that no data support a specific surveillance schedule for cholangiocarcinoma.
  • CEA and CA 19-9 are baseline tests at workup and are repeated in surveillance only as clinically indicated in NCCN; they are not assigned a fixed interval. No routine PET/CT is included in the NCCN surveillance schedule.

Sources: www.rarediseaseadvisor.com · www.staging.medscape.com · www.radmd.com
