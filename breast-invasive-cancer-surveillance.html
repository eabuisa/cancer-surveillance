Stage I–III — breast-conserving therapy

Years 1–5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical exam1–4 times per year, as clinically appropriate
Mammography (both breasts)every 12 months — begin ≥6 months after completing BCT; first mammogram 6–12 months after radiation

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical examannually
Mammographyannually
  • NCCN Breast Cancer (BINV-17 surveillance; v4.2025 / v6.2026): H&P 1–4 times per year for 5 years, then annually. ACS/ASCO survivorship guidance phrases the same cadence as every 3–6 months for years 1–3, every 6–12 months for years 4–5, then annually.
  • No routine labs (CBC, CMP), tumor markers, or systemic imaging (CT, PET/CT, bone scan) in asymptomatic patients — imaging/labs only for signs or symptoms of recurrence.
  • Annual gynecologic/pelvic exam if on tamoxifen and uterus is intact.
  • Screen for lymphedema at follow-up visits (patient education, arm measurements/bioimpedance per survivorship guidance).
  • Monitor bone density while on an aromatase inhibitor, per NCCN Survivorship.

Sources: www.ovid.com · image.e.nyulangone.org · www.statpearls.com

Stage I–III — mastectomy

Years 1–5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical exam (incl. chest wall / reconstructed breast exam)1–4 times per year, as clinically appropriate
Mammography — contralateral breastannually

After year 5

Test / examFrequency
History & physical examannually
Mammography — contralateral breastannually
  • No mammogram of the treated side after mastectomy; routine imaging of a reconstructed breast is NOT indicated (NCCN).
  • No routine labs, tumor markers, or systemic imaging (CT, PET/CT, bone scan) in asymptomatic patients.
  • Annual gynecologic/pelvic exam if on tamoxifen and uterus is intact; bone density monitoring while on an aromatase inhibitor, per NCCN Survivorship.
  • NCCN Breast Cancer v4.2025 / v6.2026 surveillance wording: H&P 1–4 times per year for 5 years, then annually (ACS/ASCO equivalent: every 3–6 months years 1–3, every 6–12 months years 4–5, then annually).

Sources: www.ovid.com · www.statpearls.com · image.e.nyulangone.org

  • Source: NCCN Breast Cancer, Principles of Monitoring Metastatic Disease (BINV-R), 'Suggested Intervals of Follow-up for Patients with Metastatic Disease.' The optimal frequency of repeat testing is uncertain and is primarily based on monitoring strategies used in clinical trials; the table is guidance and should be modified for the individual patient based on sites of disease, biology of disease, and treatment regimen. Use the same method of assessment over time (e.g., an abnormality found on chest CT should generally be monitored with repeat chest CT); RECIST/WHO criteria are encouraged.
  • There is no separate anti-HER2 column in BINV-R: targeted therapy alone or in combination (including HER2-targeted therapy) follows the chemotherapy column; endocrine therapy combined with a CDK4/6 inhibitor or other targeted therapy follows the endocrine column. BINV-R states no cardiac/LVEF monitoring interval.
  • Tumor markers (CEA, CA 15-3, CA 27.29) are 'as clinically indicated' at every time point — BINV-R states no fixed interval. Rising tumor markers are concerning for progression, but an isolated increase should rarely be used to declare progression; symptoms and serum tumor markers may be more helpful in bone-dominant metastatic disease.
  • In patients who have long-term stable disease, the frequency of monitoring can be reduced (BINV-R footnote). BINV-R contains no monitoring schedule for a treatment break or after completing chemotherapy, and no surveillance schedule for stage IV disease rendered NED or for definitively treated oligometastatic disease — those patients have no NCCN-stated cadence; do not apply the stage I–III BINV-17 schedule to them.
  • Superseded values: earlier NCCN tables (e.g., JNCCN 2012) stated endocrine visits every 2–3 months, bone scan every 4 cycles on chemotherapy, and bone scan every 4–6 months on endocrine therapy. The 2024 Clinical Advisor restatement matches the current table for endocrine visits (every 1–3 months) and chemotherapy bone scan (every 4–6 cycles) but is outdated for endocrine bone scan (it states every 4–6 months; current BINV-R, eviCore, and MIMS all state every 2–6 months).
  • Circulating tumor cells / ctDNA are not included in the NCCN Guidelines for Breast Cancer for disease assessment and monitoring (Discussion).

Sources: bookcafe.yuntsg.com · www.mims.com · www.evicore.com
