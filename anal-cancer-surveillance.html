Anal Cancer — Surveillance & Follow-Up Schedule (NCCN)

Follow-up schedules for anal cancer based on NCCN guidelines, organized by stage and treatment path, then by years since treatment.

After definitive chemoradiation

Response assessment

Test / examFrequency
Physical examination and digital rectal examination (DRE)at 8–12 weeks after completing chemoradiation
Anoscopy (with physical examination and DRE, per patient guideline)at 8–12 weeks after completing chemoradiation
Re-examination for persistent diseasere-evaluate in 4 weeks if persistent disease without progression; if regression or no progression on serial exams, continue observation and re-evaluate at 3-month intervals — persistent disease may be followed up to 6 months (regression may continue to 26 weeks) as long as there is no progressive disease

Years 1–3

Test / examFrequency
Digital rectal examination (DRE)every 3–6 months, for 5 years
Inguinal lymph node palpationevery 3–6 months, for 5 years
Anoscopyevery 6–12 months, for 3 years (when possible)
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast — or chest CT without contrast plus abdomen/pelvis MRI with contrastannually, for 3 years (stage II–III)

Years 4–5

Test / examFrequency
Digital rectal examination (DRE)every 3–6 months, for 5 years
Inguinal lymph node palpationevery 3–6 months, for 5 years
  • Surveillance above applies after complete remission following definitive chemoradiation; anoscopy and imaging stop after 3 years, while DRE and inguinal palpation continue to 5 years.
  • No routine FDG-PET/CT — FDG-PET/CT is not indicated for surveillance (ANAL-3).
  • No routine CEA or other tumor-marker surveillance is recommended for anal carcinoma.
  • Anoscopy caveat (patient guideline): after radiation, anoscopy may be too painful/uncomfortable in some patients — exam under anesthesia, less frequent anoscopy, or omission may be decided jointly.
  • Conflict flag — who gets annual imaging: the current NCCN Anal Carcinoma v2.2026 ANAL-3 table and the 2026 NCCN patient Guide 3 frame annual CT/MRI for 3 years as stage II–III disease. Several secondary sources (Medscape/eMedicine summary of NCCN, and reviews citing older NCCN versions) restrict annual chest/abdomen/pelvis CT to patients whose original disease was T3–T4 or inguinal node-positive, stating CT can be avoided in complete remission when the original staging was not T3, T4, or node-positive. The stage II–III framing from the current guideline is used here; the T3–T4/node-positive restriction reflects the older formulation.
  • Minor conflict flag — anoscopy in response assessment: the clinician ANAL-3 page phrases the 8–12 week evaluation as 'exam + DRE', while the 2026 NCCN patient guideline phrases it as physical exam with DRE and anoscopy at 8–12 weeks; both are listed above.
  • NCCN version note: verified in NCCN Anal Carcinoma clinician guideline Version 2.2026 (April 7, 2026), ANAL-3/ANAL-4, and in the 2026 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Anal Cancer, Guide 3 'Surveillance during complete remission'; DRE/inguinal palpation every 3–6 months x 5 years, anoscopy every 6–12 months x 3 years, and annual imaging x 3 years are also restated in Medscape/eMedicine and in Cancers 2025 (NCCN surveillance table).
