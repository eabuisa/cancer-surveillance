  • Brain MRI surveillance is recommended regardless of PCI status. Brain MRI is more sensitive than CT for brain metastases and is preferred; use CT head with contrast only if MRI cannot be done.
  • PCI consideration (follow-up decision after chemoradiation): for complete or partial response, consider prophylactic cranial irradiation (PCI) or MRI brain surveillance — discuss risks and benefits. PCI is not recommended in patients with poor performance status or impaired neurocognitive function, and its benefit is unclear in pathologic stage I (T1–2a, N0, M0) disease. SWOG S1827/MAVERICK is evaluating MRI surveillance ± PCI in limited and extensive stage.
  • FDG-PET/CT is not recommended for routine follow-up unless contrast CT C/A/P is contraindicated (NCCN SCLC Version 1.2024 and Version 2.2026).
  • Provide a survivorship care plan after completion of initial therapy (NCCN).
  • A new pulmonary nodule should initiate work-up for a potential new primary, not be assumed to be relapse.
  • CONFLICT on brain MRI after year 2: the NCCN Version 1.2024 update summary and the JNCCN Version 2.2022 discussion phrase brain MRI/CT as every 3–4 months in year 1 and then every 6 months (JNCCN: 'during year 2'), while NCCN Version 2.2026 Principles of Imaging (SCL-G) states explicitly every 3–4 months in year 1, every 6 months in year 2, then as clinically indicated after year 2 regardless of PCI status. The Version 2.2026 wording was used here.
  • CONFLICT / variation on CT and brain MRI intervals within the NCCN range: a 2026 MDPI review restating NCCN surveillance gives CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast and brain MRI (regardless of PCI status) every 3 months in year 1 and every 6 months in year 2, then CT every 6–12 months and MRI only as clinically indicated after year 2 — a narrower institutional restatement that sits inside the NCCN 'CT every 2–6 months, more frequently in years 1–2' range. The year 3+ CT interval above (every 6–12 months) comes from that review; NCCN itself only states CT every 2–6 months, more frequently in years 1–2 and less frequently thereafter.
  • Labs: NCCN Version 2.2026 states blood work only as clinically indicated at surveillance visits. The older Version 1.2024 surveillance page listed CBC, electrolytes, LFTs, BUN and creatinine as the visit labs — no fixed lab panel/frequency is carried in the bands above beyond 'as clinically indicated'.
  • ASCO's surveillance guideline differs in framing for curative-intent SCLC: surveillance imaging every 6 months for 2 years then annually, with brain MRI that may be used every 3 months in year 1 and every 6 months in year 2. NCCN's more frequent visit and CT schedule above was used as the primary schedule for this NCCN-based app.
  • NCCN versions cited: Small Cell Lung Cancer Version 1.2024 — September 5, 2023; Version 3.2024 — June 11, 2024 (basis for NCCN Guidelines for Patients, 2024); and Version 2.2026 — September 16, 2025.

  • Surveillance applies after completion of initial (or subsequent) therapy in patients who have responded or have stable disease; progressive disease moves to subsequent / palliative therapy, not surveillance.
  • Brain MRI surveillance is recommended regardless of PCI status. Brain MRI is preferred over CT head with contrast, which is used only if MRI cannot be done.
  • PCI consideration: for patients who respond to systemic therapy, MRI brain surveillance ± consider PCI — discuss risks and benefits of PCI versus close MRI surveillance, especially in older adults (≥60 years) and in poor performance status or impaired neurocognitive function, where PCI is not recommended. Consider thoracic RT in selected responders, especially with residual thoracic disease and low-bulk extrathoracic disease that has responded to systemic therapy.
  • FDG-PET/CT is not recommended for routine follow-up unless contrast CT C/A/P is contraindicated (NCCN SCLC Version 1.2024 and Version 2.2026).
  • Imaging for known metastases (e.g. CT neck, MRI spine) should be repeated for follow-up as clinically indicated; a new pulmonary nodule should initiate work-up for a potential new primary.
  • Provide a survivorship care plan after completion of initial therapy (NCCN).
  • GAP / imprecision in the source for body CT in later years: NCCN Principles of Imaging (Version 2.2026, SCL-G) gives one shared surveillance rule for all SCLC — most member institutions use chest CT ± abdomen/pelvis every 2–6 months, more frequently in years 1–2 and less frequently thereafter — with no separate numeric CT interval for years 4–5 or after year 5 in extensive stage. Those later bands therefore repeat that phrasing rather than a specific month interval; do not read them as a distinct guideline-stated frequency.
  • Labs: NCCN Version 2.2026 states blood work only as clinically indicated. The older Version 1.2024 surveillance page listed CBC, electrolytes, LFTs, BUN and creatinine as the visit labs for both limited and extensive stage.
  • NCCN versions cited: Small Cell Lung Cancer Version 1.2024 — September 5, 2023; Version 3.2024 — June 11, 2024 (basis for NCCN Guidelines for Patients, 2024 — its patient table lists extensive-stage visits every 2 months in year 1, every 3–4 months in years 2–3, every 6 months in years 4–5, then annually, matching the clinician guideline); and Version 2.2026 — September 16, 2025.
