  • Schedule per NCCN Ovarian Cancer, Version 4.2026 (via the NCCN Guidelines for Patients, 2026), concordant with StatPearls and published reviews.
  • CA-125 discussion point: NCCN recommends checking CA-125 (or other markers) at follow-up visits if it was elevated before treatment, but patients should be counseled that the randomized EORTC 55955 / MRC OV05 trial found NO overall-survival benefit to starting chemotherapy based on a rising CA-125 alone versus waiting for clinical recurrence, and earlier treatment meant earlier toxicity. Marker monitoring is therefore a shared decision — some patients elect symptom-based follow-up only.
  • A rising CA-125 precedes clinical recurrence by roughly 2–5 months; a confirmed rise or new symptoms should prompt CT chest/abdomen/pelvis.
  • Routine surveillance imaging is NOT recommended in asymptomatic patients with stable markers; imaging is as clinically indicated.
  • Pap smear / vaginal cytology is not part of post-treatment ovarian cancer surveillance.

Sources: www.nccn.org · www.statpearls.com · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

  • Schedule per the surveillance table attributed to NCCN Ovarian Cancer (2024) in a 2025 peer-reviewed review of malignant ovarian germ cell tumors (J Gynecol Oncol 2025;36:e108). Independently corroborated in application: a 2024 Frontiers in Oncology report applying NCCN Ovarian v2.2024 used physical exam + serum tumor markers every 2 months in years 1–2 with CT chest/abdomen/pelvis every 3–4 months in year 1 and every 4–6 months in year 2 — inside the ranges shown.
  • CONFLICT FLAGGED: the NCCN Biomarkers Compendium (as restated in 2026 payer policies) lists marker TESTING on a different cadence — every 2 months in year 1, every 3 months in year 2, every 6 months in year 3, then annually through year 10 — and older compendium versions (2023–2025) stated 'no more than every 2 months for the first 2 years, every 4 months in years 3–5, then annually after year 5.' The visit bands above follow the guideline surveillance table; if marker-only checks are scheduled between visits, the current compendium cadence applies. Verify against the current NCCN Ovarian guideline text for the individual patient.
  • Marker selection follows histology: AFP for yolk sac tumors, β-hCG for choriocarcinoma (and some dysgerminomas/embryonal carcinomas), LDH for dysgerminoma; follow markers that were elevated at diagnosis.
  • Non-dysgerminoma histotypes warrant follow-up at the more frequent end of each range (per NCCN, as noted in the 2025 review).
  • Most recurrences occur within the first 2 years, but late recurrences occur — marker follow-up extends to year 10 per the Biomarkers Compendium.

Sources: pdfs.semanticscholar.org · www.frontiersin.org · www.bcbstx.com

Ongoing surveillance

Test / examFrequency
Physical examinationevery 6–12 months if early-stage, low-risk disease; every 4–6 months if high-risk disease
Tumor markers (inhibin B, anti-Müllerian hormone; estradiol/testosterone or AFP per histology)if clinically indicated — i.e., if elevated at diagnosis; NCCN frames SCST marker surveillance as clinically indicated rather than on a fixed schedule
Imaging (CT/MRI)as clinically indicated — symptoms, examination findings, or marker elevation

Duration

Test / examFrequency
Surveillance durationlong-term / indefinite — adult granulosa cell tumors recur late, sometimes decades after treatment
  • Per NCCN Ovarian Cancer, Less Common Ovarian Histopathologies (sex cord-stromal tumors): surveillance consists of physical examination with tumor marker measurement if applicable; imaging is reserved for patients with symptoms, elevated biomarkers, or suspicious findings on physical examination (as restated in Medscape's granulosa cell tumor review citing NCCN).
  • Visit frequency by risk is per the NCCN Biomarkers Compendium as restated in 2026 payer policies: every 6–12 months for early-stage, low-risk disease and every 4–6 months for high-risk disease; the compendium states marker surveillance is 'if clinically indicated.'
  • Inhibin B and anti-Müllerian hormone are the principal markers for granulosa cell tumors; estradiol, testosterone, and AFP may apply to other SCST histologies (e.g. Sertoli-Leydig). Follow markers elevated at diagnosis.
  • Late recurrence is the defining surveillance problem in adult granulosa cell tumors — recurrences 10–20+ years after treatment are well documented, so follow-up should not stop at 5 years.
  • After fertility-sparing surgery, ESMO (not NCCN) adds pelvic ultrasound every 6 months; NCCN states no fixed imaging interval for SCST.

Sources: emedicine.medscape.com · www.bcbstx.com · assets.ctfassets.net

On chemotherapy — monitoring (on-treatment monitoring)

During chemotherapy

Test / examFrequency
Physical exam, including pelvic and rectovaginal examevery 1–3 cycles
CA-125 or other tumor markers (if initially elevated)before each cycle of chemotherapy
Imaging and blood testsas needed, to assess response and side effects
  • On-treatment monitoring, not post-treatment surveillance: this is the monitoring cadence NCCN states for patients receiving chemotherapy — the setting for most stage III/IV disease, both during primary chemotherapy and during chemotherapy for recurrence. Per the NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Ovarian Cancer (2026; based on the NCCN clinician guideline Version 4.2026), 'Testing during chemotherapy': expect a physical exam every 1 to 3 cycles (a pelvic and rectovaginal exam may be done at the same time); imaging and blood tests are ordered as needed; CA-125 or other tumor markers may be performed before each cycle of chemotherapy.
  • Clinician-guideline corroboration (NCCN Ovarian Cancer v3.2025, OV-C / OV-7): patients on chemotherapy are observed closely with blood chemistry monitoring and assessed for response during and after treatment; during and after treatment for recurrence, patients are evaluated 'as indicated' with tumor markers and repeat imaging (using modalities previously used) to document response and/or disease status. The clinician text states no fixed imaging interval on therapy — the cycle-based exam/marker cadence above comes from the NCCN patient guideline.
  • PARP-inhibitor maintenance monitoring — deliberately NOT given a numeric cadence here: NCCN (OV-C, Principles of Maintenance PARP Inhibitor Therapy) requires careful monitoring of blood counts, recommends monitoring of renal and hepatic function, and requires blood-pressure monitoring for niraparib (recommended for all PARP inhibitors), but states no interval and refers to the FDA package insert for detail. The numeric lab cadences in the drug labels (e.g., niraparib CBC weekly for the first month, then monthly for the next 11 months) are FDA-label requirements, not NCCN statements, so they are not encoded as NCCN surveillance.
  • Payer timeframes were not used: eviCore's oncology imaging guidelines (ONC-21) support CT 'every 2 cycles' on treatment and CT 'every 3 months' for measurable metastatic disease on maintenance therapy — those are eviCore's own ONC-1 general timeframes, not cadences NCCN states for ovarian cancer.

Sources: www.nccn.org · hsnow.net
