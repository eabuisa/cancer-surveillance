  • NCCN version: schedule restated from NCCN Bone Cancer Version 2.2025 (February 28, 2025; JNCCN Version 2.2025) and Version 1.2025 — HealthHelp restatements of both versions give the same intervals (every 3 months years 1–2, every 4 months year 3, every 6 months years 4–5, annually year 6 and thereafter, as clinically indicated). StatPearls' restatement (citing an earlier NCCN version) gives the identical schedule and visit content: physical exam with assessment of function, imaging of the primary/postoperative site and chest, consider PET/CT or bone scan, and CBC with additional labs as clinically indicated (e.g. alkaline phosphatase).
  • Use the same primary-site and chest imaging modalities as the initial work-up. Chest CT can be performed with or without contrast as clinically indicated; low-dose non-contrast CT is recommended for restaging (NCCN footnote). Reassess function at every visit.
  • PET/CT and/or bone scan are 'consider' items only in the NCCN surveillance text — no routine fixed interval was verified. Conflict flagged: one university lecture source suggested PET/CT or bone scan yearly; this was not adopted, as the guideline restatements say only to consider them.
  • Minor conflict flagged: one textbook restatement gave every 4–6 months in year 4 before every 6 months in year 5; the NCCN-direct value (every 6 months for years 4 and 5) is used here.
  • Late effects — as indicated only, no fixed interval banded: NCCN Principles of Bone Cancer Management recommend life-long follow-up for surveillance and treatment of late effects of surgery, radiation and chemotherapy and refer to the NCCN Survivorship guidelines. Echocardiogram after anthracyclines (doxorubicin) and audiology after cisplatin are survivorship items; no fixed interval for them in the NCCN Bone Cancer surveillance schedule could be verified from 2 sources, so they are not assigned a frequency here — monitor per the survivorship care plan.

  • NCCN version: NCCN Bone Cancer Version 2.2025 (EW-2) — the v2.2025 update modified the surveillance bullet to 'chest imaging (x-ray or CT) every 2–3 mo'. Guideline content as restated in the sources: physical examination, imaging of the primary site and chest, CBC and other laboratory studies every 2–3 months for 24 months, with increasing testing intervals after 24 months and annually after 5 years, as clinically indicated (indefinitely); consider FDG-PET/CT (head-to-toe) and/or bone scan.
  • Conflict flagged: HealthHelp restatements of NCCN Bone Cancer Version 1.2025 and Version 2.2025 simplify chest imaging to 'every 3 months' in the first 2 years, while the Version 1.2024 restatement and the v2.2025 update note give 'every 2 to 3 months'. The 2–3-month value is used here, as it matches the current NCCN wording and the American Cancer Society guidance (physical exams and imaging every 2 to 3 months for the first couple of years, then less often).
  • Years 3–5 interval: NCCN does not state a fixed month count after 24 months — its wording is only to increase intervals after 24 months and go annually after 5 years. 'Every 6 months' for years 3–5 is the operationalization used in the peer-reviewed adult Ewing follow-up review (chest CT every 3 months for 2 years, then every 6 months to year 5) and in a published NCCN-following cohort (physical exam, CBC, chest imaging and primary-site imaging every 3 months years 1–2, every 6 months years 3–5, annually thereafter).
  • Conflict flagged: the adult Ewing review recommends chest CT once or twice per year from year 5 to year 10, then by clinical indication; 'annually' beyond year 5 is used here per the NCCN wording (annually after 5 years, as clinically indicated, indefinitely).
  • Late effects — as indicated only, no fixed interval banded: NCCN Principles of Bone Cancer Management recommend life-long follow-up for late effects of surgery, radiation and chemotherapy and refer to the NCCN Survivorship guidelines. Echocardiogram after anthracyclines (doxorubicin, part of standard Ewing regimens) is a survivorship item; standard Ewing regimens do not include cisplatin, so audiology applies only if an ototoxic agent was given. No fixed interval for these in the NCCN Bone Cancer surveillance schedule could be verified from 2 sources, so none is assigned — monitor per the survivorship care plan.

Other chondrosarcoma — years 6–10 and beyond

Test / examFrequency
Physical examination, with function assessment at every visitat each surveillance visit, as clinically indicated

  • NCCN Bone Cancer v2.2025 splits chondrosarcoma surveillance into two strata (CHON-2 atypical cartilaginous tumors; CHON-3 all other covered strata; Discussion MS-10). Late metastases and recurrences after 5 years are more common in chondrosarcoma than in other sarcomas, which is why chest imaging runs yearly to a minimum of 10 years in the non-ACT stratum.
  • Conflict flag — chest imaging for atypical cartilaginous tumors: an older payer restatement (NCCN Bone v1.2024) also listed chest imaging for ACT; the current v2.2025 CHON-2 surveillance column and Discussion list physical exam and primary-site imaging only for ACT (chest imaging appears in the ACT recurrence-workup column), so no routine chest imaging is listed for ACT here.
  • Dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma may be treated as osteosarcoma and mesenchymal chondrosarcoma on the Ewing paradigm (NCCN v2.2025 pathways) — if treated that way, follow the osteosarcoma / Ewing sarcoma variants instead.
  • Chest CT can be performed with or without contrast as clinically indicated; low-dose, non-contrast CT is recommended for restaging (NCCN CHON-3 footnote).

Beyond year 5

Test / examFrequency
Physical examinationat each surveillance visit, as clinically indicated
Chest imagingannually thereafter, as clinically indicated; may include CT annually
Primary-site imaging — X-ray, MRI with and without contrast ± CT with contrastas clinically indicated, for up to 10 years
  • NCCN Bone Cancer v2.2025 CHOR-3 and Discussion (MS-14): surveillance consists of a physical exam, primary-site imaging (timing and modality as clinically indicated) for up to 10 years, and chest imaging every 6 months for 5 years and annually thereafter; chest CT (with or without contrast, as clinically indicated) may be included annually. The HealthHelp restatement of NCCN Bone Cancer v2.2025 phrases the chest item identically (every 6 months for 5 years with CT included annually, then annually).
  • NCCN states no separate fixed interval for the physical examination or for primary-site imaging in this pathway — none has been invented here.

  • Giant cell tumor of bone is a benign but locally aggressive tumor covered in NCCN Bone Cancer v2.2025 (GCTB-3; Discussion MS-24). The chest cadence reflects metastatic timing: the average time to (lung) metastasis is approximately 2–4 years.
